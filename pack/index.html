Atlas of the Build Loop

This cover is the front of the book. Behind it are four arc-packs (sixteen lessons), a quiz with eight items per lesson, and four labs about how work actually moves — from a messy problem to something you can ship without lying to yourself about what you learned. Each arc is a study pack, not a chapter heading.

Most confusion in this subject is a mode problem. Divergent thinking looks like waste to a delivery calendar. A loop looks like delay to someone who already “knows.” A prototype looks like the product to a stakeholder who wanted a demo. Mix those modes and a Tuesday standup becomes a trial.

Read it as an atlas: name the loop and run it; pick a method and write intent others can execute; generate options and make the smallest honest test; critique until spend changes, then switch that slice into delivery with operations.

Who this is for

Builders, operators, and small teams who already make things — software, services, lessons, hardware, or mixed work — and want the named methods (PDCA, OODA, Agile, Scrum, Lean Startup, Design Thinking, Double Diamond) as moves, not as branding.

It is not a PMI or Scrum.org credential, not military doctrine, and not a license to skip user harm, legal, or safety review. Educational only.

The four arcs

A — The door

Four lessons: modes, Cynefin, PDCA/OODA, BML and Double Diamond. Leave able to pick and run a loop, not only name the diagram.

B — Methods and intent

Four lessons: dual-track, Agile/Scrum/honest Waterfall, WIP and DoD, commander’s intent. A coat is a bet about information.

C — Tools

Four lessons: brainstorm protocol, generation methods, fidelity kinds, smallest honest test. Polish without a kill criterion is a sales demo.

D — Guardrails and ship

Four lessons: critique and RAT, three review types, evidence thresholds, launch and ops. Review that cannot change spend is a salon.

How to use the rest of the pack

Open Contents for the four arc-packs. Each lesson is pack-depth: mechanisms, a facilitation script, a worked example on the same onboarding case, misconceptions, and practice. The quiz is 8 items per lesson. The labs are retrieval and judgment, not extra lectures.

A reasonable path: one arc per week (four lessons + one lab), not two primer cards. Do not “Agile” a one-way-door decision because the calendar said sprint.
