An offline study pack

Atlas of the Build Loop

This cover is the front of the book. Behind it are seven lessons, a 56-question quiz, and four labs about how work actually moves — from a messy problem to something you can ship without lying to yourself about what you learned.

Most confusion in this subject is a mode problem. Divergent thinking looks like waste to a delivery calendar. A loop looks like delay to someone who already “knows.” A prototype looks like the product to a stakeholder who wanted a demo. Mix those modes and a Tuesday standup becomes a trial.

Read the pack as a field guide: name the loop, pick a method, write intent, generate options, test the riskiest assumption, review without theater, then switch from learning to delivery when the evidence says so.

Who this is for

Builders, operators, and small teams who already make things — software, services, lessons, hardware, or mixed work — and want the named methods (PDCA, OODA, Agile, Scrum, Lean Startup, Design Thinking, Double Diamond) as moves, not as branding.

It is not a PMI or Scrum.org credential, not military doctrine, and not a license to skip user harm, legal, or safety review. Educational only.

The four arcs

A — The door

Divergent vs convergent, complex vs complicated, and the named loops: PDCA, OODA, Build–Measure–Learn, Double Diamond, Design Thinking stages.

B — Methods and intent

Choose a methodology without a holy war. Write commander’s intent, non-goals, and a brief that aligns people instead of instructing every click.

C — Tools

Brainstorm with deferred judgment. Prototype at the fidelity that tests the riskiest assumption — pretotype before product.

D — Guardrails and ship

Critique, pre-mortem, steelman, user tests. Then pivot or persevere, launch, and switch from learning velocity to delivery.
