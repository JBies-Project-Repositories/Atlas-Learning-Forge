# Build–Measure–Learn and Double Diamond

## Learning Objectives
- Walk Build–Measure–Learn as a product-bet loop whose **intellectual order is Learn → Measure → Build**, not the comforting order of the name.
- Map the Design Council Double Diamond as Discover / Define / Develop / Deliver — two diverge–converge pairs — and treat it as **iterative, not linear**.
- Place common Design Thinking stages (empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test) as **named doors on the same spine**, not as a second religion or a waterfall.
- Apply the **choose-one-loop-for-the-week** rule: one named loop per slice, written on the brief, without a holy war of diagrams in the same meeting.
- Refuse a “we already know” skip of diamond 1 when the work is still a problem, and refuse a Build that has no written Learn.
- Nest a BML probe inside a diamond without mashing PDCA, OODA, BML, and diamonds into one standup.
- Close Arc A on the contractor-onboarding thread: the work is named, the loop is picked, and the week can run.

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 1: modes on the wall. Diamond 1 Discover is diverge; Define is converge. Diamond 2 Develop is diverge; Deliver is converge. BML Measure/Learn is converge against a written bet.
- Lesson 2: sandwich named. BML and diamond 1 belong on the **complex** edge (findability, pre-fill trust). Deliver-as-sequence belongs on the **complicated** cutover once specified.
- Lesson 3: you can write a Check and an Orient paragraph. A BML Measure that was not written before Build is the same injury as a Check named after Do. A diamond that cannot kill a model is an Orient-skip.
- If the work can harm people, a “Build” does not replace a specialist review. An MVP is not a waiver.

## Lesson Content

### Mechanism: what these coats are for
Build–Measure–Learn and the Double Diamond are coats for **named work**, not identities and not a stack of posters. **BML** (popularized in Lean Startup talk by Eric Ries; this pack cites the **book**, not an invented paper) treats a product idea as a **hypothesis**. The named order is Build–Measure–Learn. The intellectual order is the reverse: **Learn** (what would change our mind?) → **Measure** (what signal would we trust?) → **Build** (the smallest thing that can produce that signal). **Double Diamond**, as the Design Council publishes it in the [Framework for Innovation](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/our-resources/framework-for-innovation/), is two diverge–converge pairs: **Discover / Define** (problem) then **Develop / Deliver** (solution). Design Thinking stages are the same spine with more named doors.

The move is for a week you can actually run. Arc A’s job is to name the work and pick a loop. Lessons 1–3 gave you modes, domains, and two clocks (PDCA, OODA). This lesson adds the product-bet clock and the problem-framing diamonds, then **forces a choice**. Collecting loops is how contractor onboarding gets a portal Build, a PDCA sticker, an OODA standup, and a diamond workshop — and still DMs staff to finish the tax packet.

If you Build first because building is comforting, you have a feature factory with a blog vocabulary. If you skip Discover because “we already know,” you have a solution diamond aimed at the wrong job. If you run all four named loops in one meeting, you have a costume change.

### BML: named order vs intellectual order
The name **Build–Measure–Learn** describes the **visible** sequence once you are in motion: you put a thing in front of the world, you instrument it, you update the bet. The name is a trap if you treat it as the **planning** sequence. Intellectual order is **Learn → Measure → Build**:

1. **Learn** — write the belief and what would change your mind. “If new contractors can find the tax packet, unassisted completion in one sitting will stop requiring Slack DMs.” Or: “If we pre-fill from HRIS, they will not DM about fields.” Those are different Learns. Pick one.
2. **Measure** — write the signal you would trust, including a fail rule. “4/5 next starts find the packet from the mail in under three minutes with zero location-DMs.” Not “positive feedback.” Not “leadership likes the demo.”
3. **Build** — the smallest artifact that can produce that signal. A revised subject line and five observed starts. A paper pre-fill on a printed packet. Not an architecture. Not a vendor.

What the loop is for: falsifying a **product or usage bet** you could be wrong about. It is the complex-edge coat from Lesson 2 when the bet is “will a human do X?” It is not a coat for a statute. You do not BML the W-4. You may BML whether contractors can **find** the W-4.

Pivot or persevere is Learn’s output, not a mood. Persevere means the measure moved as predicted and the belief still earns the next Build. Pivot means the belief died and a different Learn is now on the wall. Neither is “ship more of the portal.” Feature velocity without learning velocity is Lesson 3’s panic tempo in a startup shirt.

### Double Diamond: Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver
The Design Council’s public model is two diamonds. **Diamond 1 — problem:** Discover (diverge on the problem space) then Define (converge on a problem statement a user would recognize). **Diamond 2 — solution:** Develop (diverge on ways to answer that problem) then Deliver (converge on a thing you will actually put in the world). The four words are **Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver**. They are not a waterfall you pass through once. The Design Council’s own framing is a **framework for innovation**, not a linear gate set. A failed Deliver sends you back to Define, sometimes back to Discover. A clean Define can still reopen Discover when the first five contractors refuse the problem you named.

What the diamonds are for: stopping a solution diamond aimed at the wrong job. Skipping diamond 1 is the default corporate injury. “Fix contractor onboarding” arrives as a portal (Develop) and a Friday demo (Deliver) with no Discover that watched a person fail, and no Define that a contractor would recognize (“I cannot find the tax packet in the welcome mail, so I DM staff”). Lesson 1’s 20/15 is how you run Discover and then a first Define. Lesson 2 tells you not to Discover a statute. Lesson 3 tells you that Deliver without a Check is vibes.

Iterate, not linear. The diagram looks left-to-right. The work is not. You may loop Discover twice in a week. You may Deliver a mail-subject change while still Discovering pre-fill trust. You may not claim you are “in Deliver” because a slide template has four chevrons and the fourth is colored in.

### Design Thinking stages as named doors on the same spine
A common teaching set — empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test — is **not a second spine**. Labels vary by school. In this atlas they are doors on the diamonds:

- **Empathize** is a Discover door: watch, listen, sit with the contractor who cannot find the packet. It is not a persona workshop that never meets a human.
- **Define** is the Define door: problem statement, How Might We, non-goals. Lesson 1’s converge criterion lives here.
- **Ideate** is a Develop door: structurally different options, deferred judgment.
- **Prototype** is still Develop (and sometimes a small Deliver): the smallest artifact that can be tested. Fidelity is Lesson 11’s job; here, do not climb fidelity to impress a demo.
- **Test** is the hinge that sends you back: task success, a BML Measure, a PDCA Check. Failed test → Define or Discover, not a blame meeting.

What the stages are for: giving a room named doors so it can say which one is legal this hour. What they are not for: a certification, a mural, or a waterfall that “finishes Design Thinking” before “real work.” Engineers skip diamond 1 at the same cost as designers. Ops skips it when it ships an office hour as the product vision. Leadership skips it when it orders a demo.

If your program uses different stage names (explore, frame, make, validate), map them onto the spine and stop arguing brands. Coats, not identities.

### Decision rule: choose one loop for the week
Decision rule, not a personality test and not a poster wall:

1. If you cannot state the problem in one sentence a user would recognize → **Double Diamond / Design Thinking**, and you are still in diamond 1 (Discover/Define, empathize/define). Do not Build.
2. If you have a product or usage bet you could be wrong about, and the problem is already user-recognizable → **Build–Measure–Learn**, with Learn and Measure written before Build.
3. If a process exists and you can measure it → **PDCA** (Lesson 3), Check named in Plan. Do not rebrand it as BML to look modern.
4. If the environment or stakeholder weather is moving faster than your meetings, and the model is the thing at risk → **OODA** (Lesson 3), protect Orient. Do not rebrand it as “lean.”
5. **Choose one loop for the week** per named slice. Write it on the brief. You may nest (BML probe inside diamond 1; PDCA inside a BML Measure; OODA around demo weather). You may not run a holy war of diagrams in the same meeting. Mixing PDCA “check” language with a brainstorm, or coloring Deliver while still arguing the problem, is fake rigor.

The choose-one rule is the close of Arc A. Name the work (Lessons 1–2). Pick a loop (Lessons 3–4). Run it. A week that “does all of them” does none of them. A month may use more than one. A meeting may not.

The test of the rule is a week card a competent teammate could run without the slide deck. Slice, domain, one loop name, intellectual first step, and a list of costumes that are illegal until the next observe date. If the card still says “BML + diamonds + OODA (and PDCA if we have time),” you have not chosen. You have insured yourself against being wrong about the diagram, which is the opposite of running a loop.

### Facilitation script: pick and run (45 minutes)
One facilitator. Inputs on the table: Lesson 1 artifact (keep/park/kill), Lesson 2 sandwich, Lesson 3 model paragraph if one exists. This meeting is **converge on loop choice**, then **write the first card**. It is not a new generate unless diamond 1 is explicitly chosen and time-boxed.

**0:00–5:00 — Name the slice.** One slice only. “Findability of the tax packet,” or “pre-fill trust,” or “payroll dry run.” If someone says “onboarding,” split or cancel. Write domain (complicated/complex) and mode (this hour is decide-the-loop, not ideate).

**5:00–12:00 — User-recognizable problem test.** Can a contractor recognize the sentence? “I cannot find the packet” passes. “We need a unified portal experience” fails. If it fails, you are in diamond 1. Loop choice is already made: Discover/Define this week. Skip to 28:00 and write the Discover card (who we will watch, by when). Do not entertain BML Builds.

**12:00–20:00 — If the problem passed, write Learn before any Build.** Two columns: BELIEF and WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR MIND. One belief. For pre-fill: “Pre-fill from existing HRIS will cut field-DMs without raising error-DMs.” Falsifier: “If 3/5 still DM about fields, or if 2/5 contest the pre-fill as wrong, the bet dies.” If the group cannot write the falsifier, they are not in BML. They are in Deliver theater.

**20:00–28:00 — Measure then smallest Build.** Measure: numbers, window, fail rule. Build: the cheapest artifact that can produce the measure. Paper packet with handwritten pre-fill is legal. Vendor is not (non-goal). Architecture is not. Leadership clickable is not unless it is the actual test.

**28:00–38:00 — Choose-one card.** Fill a one-page week card:

- Slice and domain
- Loop this week (one name)
- Intellectual first step (Learn / Discover / Check / Orient)
- What we will not do (the other loops’ costumes)
- Next observe date

Read it aloud. If two loop names remain, you failed the rule. Nesting must be written as outer/inner, not as “and.”

**38:00–45:00 — Kill the costume list.** Every already-scheduled artifact (Friday demo, portal mock, vendor call, welcome video) must point at the card or die for the week. This is Lesson 3’s task-list-vs-model move applied to loop choice.

Cancel if a senior person demands “show all four loops in the steering pack.” That request is identity. Give them the one-page card or give them no session.

### Iterate, do not linearize
Linearization is the injury that looks like professionalism. Coloring in Discover-Define-Develop-Deliver on a gated project plan, never going left. Running empathize-define-ideate-prototype-test once per fiscal year. Treating BML as a three-sprint waterfall (sprint 1 Build, sprint 2 Measure, sprint 3 Learn) so Learn arrives after the launch date. Treating choose-one as choose-forever.

Iteration tells:

- A test that fails **returns** to Define (“we named the wrong job”) or Discover (“we never saw the job”).
- A Measure that kills a belief **rewrites Learn** before the next Build.
- A complicated Deliver (payroll file) **stays sequential** and does not pretend to iterate the statute; the complex edge around it may still loop.
- Diamond 2 without diamond 1 is not “being iterative.” It is skipping.

The Design Council model is useful because the diamonds make the skip visible. You can see that you are in the right-hand diamond with an unearned problem. Design Thinking stages are useful because Test is a named door back. BML is useful because Learn is supposed to be the point. None of them are useful as a mural you walk past on the way to the demo.

### Failure catalog
**Build first because building comforts.** Architecture for pre-fill before anyone found the packet. Feature factory with Lean vocabulary.

**Measure as applause.** Demo frowns, survey smiles, stakeholder excitement. Not a signal you named when you were honest.

**Learn as a slide of quotes.** Quotes without a falsifier. A quote can be Observe. It is not yet Learn.

**Skip diamond 1.** “We already know.” Solution diamond at the wrong job. Portal for a mail problem.

**Linear diamonds.** Four chevrons, one pass, launch. The diagram lied; the work needed a loop.

**Design Thinking as a caste.** “The designers will empathize; engineering will deliver.” Coats, not identities. The same trio can watch a contractor and then specify a file.

**All loops, one meeting.** PDCA Check language in a diverge, OODA tempo to skip Orient, BML to justify a Build, diamonds on the slide. Costume change.

**BML on a statute.** Prototyping the W-4. Learning theater on a one-way door.

**Choose-one as branding.** Picking BML because it sounds startup, while the slice is a dry-run file that needed PDCA.

**Deliver as demo.** A leadership artifact that cannot fail is not Deliver and not Measure. It is a costume.

Uncertainty kept in the body: BML is book-and-practice talk (Ries and the Lean Startup lineage), not a paper this pack will invent. Double Diamond origin stories and vendor overlays are contested; this atlas uses the Design Council’s public Framework for Innovation as the home of the four words and the two diamonds. Design Thinking stage lists vary by school. Teach the **spine** and the **choose-one rule**, not a trademarked workshop.

## Worked example(s)
**Problem:** Arc A, week of the leadership demo. Lesson 1 kept findability, pre-fill, and office hour. Lesson 2 split the sandwich: payroll cutover complicated; portal/findability complex. Lesson 3 Orient killed “portal chrome is the bottleneck” and named a PDCA Check on the welcome mail. Leadership still wants a clickable Deliver. An engineer wants to Build pre-fill “because we will need it anyway.” A designer has a Double Diamond slide with Deliver colored in. Ops offers to “OODA the week.” The non-goal remains no new vendor. The tax packet still exists as a statute.

**Steps:**
1. Name the slice for the **complex** week: findability of the tax packet. (Payroll dry run keeps its PDCA from Lesson 3 on a separate card. Two slices, two cards, choose-one on each.)
2. User-recognizable problem test. Contractor sentence: “I cannot find the tax packet in the welcome mail, so I Slack-DM staff.” Passes. Diamond 1 Define is **earned enough to run a bet**, but only on findability — not on pre-fill, not on a portal. Pre-fill is a different Learn and is parked until findability’s Measure moves.
3. Choose one loop for this slice this week: **Build–Measure–Learn**, nested inside diamond 1’s Test door. Outer name on the card: BML. Inner: the Build is a Discover instrument (revised mail), not a Deliver of a product. Write that nesting so nobody can claim Deliver is colored in.
4. Intellectual order, written before anyone opens a design file. **Learn:** “The reason contractors DM staff is that they cannot find the packet; if they can find it, unassisted completion of the statutory fields will not require a location-DM.” **Measure:** 4/5 next starts find the packet from the revised mail in under three minutes with zero location-DMs; fail rule: 2/5 or more still fail to find it. **Build:** subject line “Tax packet — 25 minutes, due day one,” packet link in sentence one, five observed starts, no portal, no vendor, no pre-fill yet.
5. Kill costumes against the card. Clickable leadership demo dies as a Build (it cannot produce the Measure and cannot fail honestly). Pre-fill architecture dies for the week (wrong Learn). Double Diamond slide may stay on the wall as a map if and only if Discover/Define are the lit doors; Deliver coloring is removed. “OODA the week” is refused as a synonym; if the demo date jumps again, run Lesson 3’s Orient script **around** this card, do not replace it.
6. Complicated slice, separate card, different loop: payroll dry run remains **PDCA** with the named Check from Lesson 3. That is choose-one **per slice**, not choose-one for the company. Do not BML the file format.
7. After the five starts, Learn for real. If Measure passes, diamond 1 can Define the next problem (“I found it but field 12 makes me DM”) and a new week may choose BML on pre-fill. If Measure fails, you are still in Discover — maybe the packet is in the mail and still unfindable on a phone, maybe the contractor never opens mail — and you do **not** “iterate” into a portal Deliver. You iterate left.

**Answer / result:** Arc A has done its job. The work is named: findability first, cutover separate, pre-fill parked, portal not the bottleneck. The loop is picked and runnable: **BML this week on the complex slice, Learn written first; PDCA this week on the complicated dry run.** The leadership artifact is the week card plus the Measure, not a clickable costume. The atlas can now hand this thread to Arc B (coats, intent, dual-track) without having only drawn a map. You can run the loop.

## Misconception inventory
| Misconception | Reality |
|---------------|---------|
| “Build–Measure–Learn means build first.” | Intellectual order is Learn → Measure → Build. The name is the visible motion, not the plan. |
| “Double Diamond is a four-stage waterfall.” | It is two loops. Iterate. A failed Deliver returns to Define or Discover. |
| “Design Thinking is only for designers.” | It is a problem-framing spine. Engineers and ops skip diamond 1 at the same cost. |
| “We already know the problem, so skip to Deliver.” | That sentence is the usual death of diamond 1. Prove a user-recognizable problem. |
| “Using every loop in one week is mature.” | Choose one per slice. A holy war of diagrams hides the skipped step. |
| “A leadership demo is a Measure.” | If it cannot fail, it is not a Measure. Applause is not a signal. |
| “Pre-fill should be built now because we will need it.” | That is Build without Learn. Findability is a different bet. |
| “PDCA, OODA, BML, and diamonds are interchangeable words for Agile.” | Same spine, different jobs. Pick the coat that matches this week’s slice. |
| “BML can falsify a statute.” | Do not pretotype a one-way legal door. BML is for usage bets, not W-4 contents. |
| “Once we Choose BML we can never PDCA.” | Choose-one is per week per slice. Domain shifts can change the coat. Nesting is legal when named. |

## Summary
- BML’s intellectual order is Learn → Measure → Build; the name is not the plan.
- Double Diamond is Discover / Define / Develop / Deliver; two pairs; iterate, do not linearize.
- Design Thinking stages are doors on that spine, not a caste or a second religion.
- Skip diamond 1 and you will Deliver the wrong job. Build first and you will Measure a costume.
- Choose one loop for the week per named slice; nest only with outer/inner written.
- Complicated cutover keeps PDCA; complex findability can run BML; demo weather may need OODA around the card — not as synonyms.
- Arc A closes when the work is named and a loop is actually running.

### Near transfer
A trio has a Double Diamond slide with Deliver colored in, a half-built pre-fill service, and no task-success number on packet findability. Leadership wants the portal on Wednesday. Write the week card: slice, loop, Learn, Measure, Build. What dies for the week?

### Far transfer
You are choosing a loop for the next two days of **this atlas**. Is “which practice item readers fail” a BML bet, a PDCA process, an OODA weather system, or still diamond 1? Choose one for the week and write Learn or Check or Orient — not all three. What would a costume version of this choice look like?

## Difficulty tiers
- **Novice:** You can say Learn → Measure → Build and you can name the four diamond words as two pairs.
- **Working:** You can fill a week card, refuse a demo-as-Measure, park pre-fill until findability’s Learn is done, and keep PDCA on the cutover.
- **Expert:** You nest a BML probe inside diamond 1 without coloring Deliver, run choose-one in front of leadership, and close Arc A with named work plus a loop that is actually in motion.

## Domain scaffolds
Professional building work: the brief’s first lines are mode (L01), domain (L02), loop-for-the-week (L03–L04). Contractor onboarding continues as the sandwich: complicated payroll/tax cutover on PDCA; complex findability on BML inside diamond 1; pre-fill as a later Learn; leadership demo as a costume unless it is the Measure. Safety-critical and statute-shaped slices do not get a Build-first coat. Arc B will pick delivery coats; it cannot save a week that never chose a loop.

## Learn more

- [Manifesto for Agile Software Development](https://agilemanifesto.org/) — working software and responding to change are not a license to skip diamond 1 or to Build before Learn.
- [Principles behind the Agile Manifesto](https://agilemanifesto.org/principles.html) — inspect and adapt; write what you would inspect (Measure/Check) first.
- [The 2020 Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html) — a done increment is Deliver only if the problem was earned; empiricism is not a mural of loops.
- [Snowden & Boone, “A Leader’s Framework for Decision Making,” HBR November 2007](https://hbr.org/2007/11/a-leaders-framework-for-decision-making) — BML and diamond 1 sit on complex slices; do not probe a one-way complicated door.
- [Nielsen, 10 Usability Heuristics (NN/g)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) — expert overlay after an artifact exists; not a substitute for Learn or for Discover.
- Build–Measure–Learn — Ries / Lean Startup **book** talk; intellectual order Learn → Measure → Build. No paper invented here.
