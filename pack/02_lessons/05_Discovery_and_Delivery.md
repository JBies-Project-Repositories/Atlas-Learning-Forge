# Discovery, Delivery, and Dual-Track

## Learning Objectives
- State what discovery produces (a **decision** about what is worth building) and what delivery produces (an **increment** that meets a Definition of Done).
- Split a live sandwich into a complicated core and a complex edge without putting both on one board.
- Assign **separate capacities** to discovery and delivery that still talk on a named artifact, not on vibes.
- Refuse the two common dual-track costumes: two teams that never meet, and one team that only delivers.
- Run a 30-minute capacity-split facilitation that names a discovery floor for this cadence.
- Catch the failure where a backlog of unvalidated guesses is treated as committed delivery work.
- Write two Definitions of Done when the sandwich has two kinds of “done.”

## Prerequisites
- Arc A (L01–L04): you can name diverge vs converge, complicated vs complex, and pick a loop for the week.
- You have one live program that mixes a known sequence (a cutover, a statute, a file format) with a product bet nobody has watched a user fail at.
- No Scrum.org, PMI, or vendor credential is required. This lesson is a coat-choice, not a certification.

## Lesson Content

### Two products of work, not two moods
Discovery and delivery are not personality types, job titles, or how enthusiastic the standup sounds. They are two **products**. Discovery’s product is a **decision** about what is worth building — or worth killing, or worth not knowing yet. Delivery’s product is an **increment** that exists in the world at a quality bar you can fail. If you cannot name which product today’s hours are for, you are collecting activity.

The move is for **information hygiene**. Complex work (Arc A) still has unknown cause and effect; you need contact with a user, a demand signal, a usability failure, or a risk before you spend delivery capacity. Complicated work already has known cause and effect; spending weeks “discovering” a payroll file format that the statute already specified is theater. Most real programs are a **sandwich**: a complex edge sitting on a complicated core. The injury is running the whole sandwich as one stream so that unvalidated guesses get “committed” and known sequences get “iterated” until the legal date is a surprise.

A methodology coat (L06) sits on top of this split. Agile values, Scrum empiricism, and honest sequence are all unusable if discovery and delivery have been mashed into one board named after the department. Dual-track is not a third religion. It is the org chart of the information.

### Discovery produces a decision
**Discovery** is work whose output can be read as a sentence with a verb of judgment: build this, do not build that, we still do not know X and here is the next probe. Interviews, pretotypes, spikes, service safaris, paper maps of an inbox, and “how might we” sessions are discovery **methods**. They are not the product. A wall of notes with no decision is unfinished discovery, the same way untested code is unfinished delivery.

Four decision types show up in this atlas. A **problem** decision names who fails, at which job, with what evidence — without a solution noun. A **demand** decision says whether anyone will use or pay for a proposed slice. A **usability** decision says whether a person can complete a named task on the current path. A **risk** decision says whether a constraint (legal, safety, vendor, accessibility) makes a slice dishonest. You do not need all four every week. You need the one that is currently pretending to be known.

The decision rule for calling work “discovery” is simple: **if this hour cannot change what we will build, it is not discovery.** Decorating a backlog with story points is not a decision. Adding seven epics named after leadership nouns (“AI summaries,” “nicer onboarding”) is a backlog dump. Discovery has succeeded when a competent teammate could act on the sentence without you in the room: “Findability is untested and is the next probe; pre-fill is not earned; the payroll file is not a discovery problem.” That sentence is already halfway to commander’s intent (L08).

### Delivery produces an increment to Done
**Delivery** is work whose output can be used without a narrator. The [2020 Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html) calls that output an **Increment**: a concrete, additive, usable stepping stone, and work cannot be part of an Increment unless it meets the **Definition of Done**. This lesson uses that meaning even when you are not wearing Scrum. If “done” means “we talked,” “the slide exists,” or “QA will catch it later,” you do not have delivery. You have a status meeting with a build server.

Delivery is for **known slices** — work discovery already earned, or work that was never a bet (the complicated core). Its steering wheel is cadence, scope, WIP, and Definition of Done (L07). You do not need those words to start; you need the failure they prevent: eight half-built features, a demo of undone work, and a quality bar that cannot fail.

The decision rule: **if this hour cannot produce or finish something a user or a statute could touch, it is not delivery.** Refactors, tests, and accessibility fixes can be delivery when they are what the increment needs to meet Done. They are not delivery when they are how the team hides from a missing decision. A leadership demo of a pre-fill model that no contractor can find is not an increment. It is a costume of delivery on an unearned bet.

### Dual-track is two capacities that still talk
**Dual-track discovery and delivery** is a way to run both products in the same cadence without pretending they are the same meeting. One track probes. The other builds what probing already earned. The tracks have **separate capacities** — named people-hours or named bodies this week — because leftover-time discovery is how discovery dies. Delivery always looks more urgent. Leadership always has a demo date. If discovery is funded with “after the sprint,” it will not happen.

The tracks **talk**. They are not two teams who never meet, and they are not one team that only delivers. Talking means a named artifact moves: a decision memo, a killed bet, an earned slice with a DoD, a constraint that makes the earned slice impossible. Talking does not mean a combined Kanban that hides the legal date behind story points, or a “discovery standup” that reports busyness. If the people building the increment cannot say what decision earned it, the tracks have stopped talking. If the people probing cannot say what delivery learned from the last increment, they are discovering in a vacuum.

Capacity is a floor, not a vibe. A practical starting split on a sandwich is not a universal ratio; it is a sentence: “This two-week cadence, two people-days are discovery-only (findability probe), the rest of engineering is delivery-only on the payroll rehearsal and on slices already earned, and we will not start pre-fill.” Write the floor in the brief. When someone “pulls a discoverer onto the demo,” you are not being flexible. You are deleting the track.

### Sandwich projects: a complex edge on a complicated core
A **sandwich** is the usual live program: a **complicated** core with known cause and effect (a payroll file that must match a statute on a date, an identity-provider integration that already exists, a tax packet whose fields are already law) plus a **complex** edge (will a new contractor find the packet, complete it on a phone, stop Slack-DMing staff?). Arc A already gave the tell: if you run the same steps next month, do you expect the same result? Yes → core. No → edge.

The sandwich is why dual-track exists as an org-chart of information rather than as a staffing fad. The core wants sequence, rehearsal, and a DoD you can dry-run. The edge wants probes, a decision clock, and a different DoD (task success, not file-match). Put both on one board named “HR Digital” and the board will lie in both directions: the legal date will look like a story, and the product bet will look like a Gantt.

Decision rule for the sandwich: **split the work by information problem before you split the people.** You may have one team wearing two coats in different hours. You may not have one coat for the whole department. Two DoDs is normal. Two cadences is often honest (daily risk for the cutover, weekly decision review for the portal). Two teams that never meet is the costume this lesson forbids: the cutover people will not hear that contractors cannot find the packet, and the portal people will not hear that Thursday’s file must not change.

### Decision rule: what goes on which track this week
The rule is for the **next week**, not for the department’s identity. You run it on a wall with the live inventory from the sandwich split. Each item gets a track because of its information problem, not because of who sits where. If two people would classify the same sticky differently, you do not yet have a shared tell — go back to Arc A’s question (same steps next month, same result?). Until that is shared, dual-track is a slogan.

Use the following as a wall script, not as a personality test.

1. If cause and effect are known and the output is specified (statute, file format, cutover date) → **delivery by sequence**, with a DoD and a rehearsal. Do not “discover” it. Do not apologize for a plan.
2. If you do not yet know the job, the demand, the usability, or the risk → **discovery**, and the output this cadence is a decision, not a coded slice.
3. If discovery already earned a slice and you can ship an increment to Done → **delivery on that slice only**. Do not quietly add the next guess.
4. If leadership named a noun (“AI,” “portal,” “nicer”) and no user has been watched failing the current job → the noun is **not** a delivery item. It is a discovery candidate or a non-goal.
5. If someone wants one board, one method, and one DoD for the sandwich → they are buying identity. Split the sandwich anyway.

You may run both (1) and (2) in the same week. You may not run them in the same meeting with the same success metric. A useful tell: the discovery review asks “what did we decide?” The delivery review asks “what is Done?” If both meetings ask “how busy were we?,” neither track exists. Write the week’s answers into the brief (L08) the same day; a rule that lives only on a whiteboard will lose to the demo date by Thursday.

### Failure catalog
**Backlog as decision.** Unvalidated epics are loaded as “committed” work. Discovery was skipped; delivery will invent the product in the review. Repair: every delivery item must point at a dated decision sentence.

**Discovery as lifestyle.** Probes continue because they are interesting. No kill, no earn, no “we still don’t know X.” Repair: a decision clock on the cadence (L07). A week of discovery with no sentence is a failed week, not a gentle one.

**Two teams that never meet.** A “research” group and a “build” group with no shared brief. Decisions do not reach builders; constraints do not reach probers. Repair: one brief, one talk artifact, overlapping humans at the inspect.

**One team that only delivers.** Discovery is “the PM’s job in leftover hours.” The board is full. Repair: a discovery floor in people-hours, protected like production support.

**Combined board named after the department.** Payroll date and portal guesses share ranks. Repair: two views or two DoDs, even if the staffing is shared.

**Demo as increment.** Leadership is shown undone work because the date arrived. The [Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html) is blunt: if it does not meet Done, it is not an Increment and is not presented as one. Repair: show the decision (findability untested) rather than a pretty fake.

**Sandwich denial.** The whole program is declared Agile or the whole program is declared a rollout. Repair: label each live item core or edge before the next planning meeting.

**Capacity theft.** A demo date “temporarily” eats the discovery floor. Temporary becomes the culture. Repair: treat a stolen discovery day as a missed inspect, the same way you would treat a skipped production check.

### Facilitation: 30-minute dual-track capacity split
This is a working session, not a methodology briefing. Invite the people who will actually do the hours this cadence, plus whoever currently owns the demo date. Put the sandwich on a wall under two headers: **core (complicated)** and **edge (complex)**. Ban method names for the first ten minutes.

**0:00–0:05 — Inventory.** List every live item in the program (contractor onboarding, tax packet, pre-fill, AI helper, payroll file cutover, leadership demo). One sticky per item. No grouping yet. If an item has no owner, it still goes on the wall; unnamed work is how WIP explodes later.

**0:05–0:12 — Label the sandwich.** For each sticky, the room answers one question only: *If we run the same steps next month, do we expect the same result?* Yes → core. No → edge. Unknown → edge until proven. Do not argue coats. Payroll cutover is core. “Will contractors find the packet?” is edge. “AI summaries” is edge or a non-goal; it is not core.

**0:12–0:20 — Capacity floor.** Write this cadence’s calendar (usually one or two weeks). Name discovery hours as a **floor**, not a leftover. A small team might write “two people-days, named humans, blocked on calendars.” Delivery hours get the rest, minus the cutover rehearsal. If the demo date requires stealing the floor, the honest move is to **cut demo scope**, not to pretend discovery will happen at night.

**0:20–0:26 — Talk protocol.** Invent nothing fancy. One artifact moves between tracks: a dated decision sentence. Examples that pass: “Findability is the next probe; five contractors; task = finish the packet from their real inbox; success bar written before the sessions.” “Pre-fill is not earned.” “Payroll file DoD is a dry-run match to the statute; not a sprint-review toy.” Examples that fail: a Jira epic, a slide, “we’re aligned.”

**0:26–0:30 — Kill the lying board.** If a single board currently ranks the legal date next to AI ideas, split the view or write two DoDs on the same view in letters large enough to insult. Read the discovery floor and the talk artifact aloud. If you need a narrator, you did not finish.

After the thirty minutes, someone types the sentences into the brief (L08). The facilitation has failed if the only output is a new column named “Discovery.”

### What “talk” looks like when it is working
A working dual-track has boring artifacts. Discovery writes a decision with a date, a who, a task, and a bar. Delivery writes an increment against a DoD, or writes “cannot meet DoD because of constraint C.” Both land in the same brief. Neither track is allowed to start a third thing because the other is “blocked.” Blocked delivery does not automatically become a new discovery project; it becomes a constraint in the brief. Blocked discovery does not automatically become coding; it becomes a missing decision, which is already information.

The [Design Council Double Diamond](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/our-resources/framework-for-innovation/) is the map underneath: Discover and Define are diamond 1 (problem); Develop and Deliver are diamond 2 (solution). Dual-track is how you stop diamond 2 from eating diamond 1 in organizations that fund only “build.” It is also how you stop diamond 1 from never ending. Design Council is explicit that the diamonds are not a linear once-through; testing early can be part of discovery, and delivery still learns. Dual-track is the capacity version of that sentence.

**Uncertainty:** popular writing on “dual-track agile” is a practitioner pattern (parallel discovery and delivery), not a standard with a single public spec in this pack’s sources. This atlas teaches the **move**: two products, two capacities, one talk artifact, sandwich honesty. It does not teach a vendor scaling layer, and it does not treat dual-track as a reason to hire two teams who email each other quarterly.

## Worked example(s)
**Problem:** A department wants “Agile” for a payroll cutover with a fixed legal date, plus a new contractor self-service portal nobody has watched a human fail at. Leadership also asked for “AI summaries / nicer onboarding” and a demo. The current pain, from Arc A, is specific: new contractors cannot finish required tax/onboarding forms without Slack-DMing staff. Friday’s honest artifact was a loop, not a six-month Gantt. This week someone opened one Jira board named “HR Digital” and loaded payroll, the portal, pre-fill, the AI helper, and the demo onto it as Sprint 1.

**Steps:**
1. Refuse the single board as a decision. Inventory the live items. Label each core or edge. Payroll-file cutover: complicated, one-way door, statute-shaped → **core**. Portal findability, pre-fill, AI helper: complex, cause and effect not known in advance → **edge**. “Nicer onboarding” is not an item; it is a complaint.
2. Name the two products. Discovery this cadence must produce a **decision** about the edge: what is worth building first. Delivery this cadence must produce an **increment** on the core (a payroll dry-run that can fail a DoD) and must not pretend the AI slide is an increment.
3. Write the discovery decision you actually need. You do **not** yet know whether contractors can find the packet. You do **not** yet know pre-fill quality. You do know staff-DMing is the pain. So the discovery sentence is: “This cadence, the probe is findability from the real inbox → link → form. Pre-fill is not earned. AI helper is a non-goal this round.” That is a decision, not a prototype plan dressed as certainty.
4. Split capacities. Two people-days, named, calendar-blocked, are discovery-only for the findability probe design (the test itself is Arc C). Remaining engineering time is delivery on the payroll rehearsal. Nobody is “the portal team” who never hears about Thursday’s file. Nobody is “the payroll team” who never hears that the packet cannot be found.
5. Write two DoDs on the wall. Payroll DoD: “file matches the statute in a dry run; rollback named; no cute fake.” Portal increment DoD (for when a slice is earned): “a contractor can start the packet from their phone without a staff DM.” Do not use one Done column.
6. Name the talk artifact. Discovery will hand delivery a dated sentence, not a stack of tickets. Delivery will hand discovery the constraint list from the identity provider and the cutover freeze window. The leadership demo, if it happens, shows the **split and the decision**, not an unearned pre-fill model.
7. Kill the costume. If the board cannot show the legal date without ranking it against AI stories, split the view. If someone says “we’ll discover in the sprint review,” they have already collapsed the tracks.

**Answer / result:** Two products, one department, one brief. Discovery is funded as a floor and has a decision to make (findability first; pre-fill unearned; AI not this round). Delivery is honest about the complicated cutover and is forbidden from minting portal features that were not earned. The tracks still meet: same humans, same constraints, different outputs. Dual-track here is not two teams. It is the sandwich made visible. Arc B still does not know pre-fill quality; it has refused to build it anyway.

## Key Terms
| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Discovery | Work that produces a decision about what is worth building, killing, or still not knowing |
| Delivery | Work that produces a usable increment that meets a Definition of Done |
| Dual-track | Discovery and delivery in the same cadence with separate capacities that still talk |
| Talk artifact | The dated decision, earned slice, or constraint note that moves between tracks |
| Sandwich | A program with a complicated core and a complex edge |
| Complicated core | Known cause and effect; sequence, rehearsal, and a dry-run DoD are honest |
| Complex edge | Cause and effect clearer after probes; discovery must earn the slice |
| Discovery floor | Named hours this cadence that delivery is not allowed to steal |
| Backlog dump | Unvalidated guesses loaded as if they were committed delivery |
| Increment | A concrete, usable stepping stone; not a slide, not a talk |
| Definition of Done | The fail-able quality bar that makes “done” mean the same thing |
| Capacity theft | Demo or delivery urgency eating the discovery floor “just this once” |

## Misconception inventory
| Misconception | Reality |
|---------------|---------|
| “Discovery is research; delivery is engineering.” | Both are products of work. Engineers discover; researchers can deliver a decision. Titles are not tracks. |
| “Dual-track means two teams.” | Two teams that never meet is the costume. Dual-track is two capacities that talk. |
| “If we are experienced we can skip discovery.” | Experience without contact still guesses. Dual-track exists because experience lies. |
| “A filled backlog means we decided.” | A backlog dump is unvalidated work wearing a rank. |
| “Discovery output is a prototype.” | Prototype is a method. Output is a decision (including “kill” or “still unknown”). |
| “Delivery output is a demo.” | Delivery output meets Done and can be used without a narrator. Demos of undone work are not increments. |
| “One DoD can cover payroll and the portal.” | Two kinds of true need two bars. File-match is not task-success. |
| “Discovery can live in leftover hours.” | Leftovers are how the track dies. Write a floor. |
| “Putting both on one Scrum board will keep us aligned.” | One board often hides the legal date and the guess in the same ranking. Alignment is a brief, not a column. |
| “Dual-track is a scaling framework.” | This pack does not teach vendor scaling. The move is local: two products, one sandwich. |

## Summary
- Discovery produces a **decision**; delivery produces an **increment** to Done. Mood and job title are irrelevant.
- Dual-track is separate capacities that still talk on a named artifact. It is not two teams who never meet, and not one team that only delivers.
- Most programs are sandwiches: complicated core plus complex edge. Split by information problem before you split people.
- A discovery floor is a decision. Leftover-time discovery is a wish.
- One board, one DoD, and one demo date for the whole sandwich is how both tracks lie.
- Unearned nouns (AI helper, pre-fill, “nicer”) stay out of delivery until a dated sentence earns them.
- The talk artifact is boring on purpose: a decision, an earned slice, a constraint — readable without a narrator.

### Near transfer
Your team runs two-week sprints. The board has payroll cutover, a portal redesign, and “AI onboarding” in one Sprint Backlog. Discovery is “the designer’s Fridays.” Write: (a) the sandwich split, (b) a discovery floor in hours, (c) the decision discovery owes this cadence, (d) what delivery is forbidden to start.

### Far transfer
A hospital wants dual-track for a known infusion-pump firmware patch (safety, one-way door) and a new patient-messaging app nobody has tested. How do you split capacities without two teams that never meet, and what would be malpractice to put in the same Done column?

## Spaced retrieval notes
- Day 0: label every live item on your current program discovery, delivery, or unlabeled. Unlabeled is a finding.
- Day 2: write one dated decision sentence your discovery track owes this week. If you have no discovery track, that is the note.
- Day 7: count hours actually spent on discovery vs the floor you claimed. Name one theft if it happened.
- Day 21: catch a demo, board, or standup where an unearned guess was treated as committed delivery. Write the repair in one sentence.

## Difficulty tiers
- **Novice:** say what each track produces; label a sandwich; refuse one combined Done column.
- **Working:** run the 30-minute capacity split; protect a discovery floor; name a talk artifact the rest of the team can read.
- **Expert:** keep dual-track alive for a quarter without delivery starving discovery and without discovery never deciding; restaff hours without creating two teams that never meet.

## Domain scaffolds
Professional teams: put the track name on the brief only after the information problem. Safety, payroll, and legal one-way doors default to sequenced delivery plus rehearsal even if the rest of the org is “doing discovery.” Healthcare, finance, and law: a discovery probe must not fake a one-way door (do not Wizard-of-Oz a statutory filing). If the domain can harm people, discovery decisions include “we will not probe this in production.”
