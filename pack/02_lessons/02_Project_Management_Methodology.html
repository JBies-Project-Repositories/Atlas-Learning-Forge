Project Management Methodology

Learning Objectives

  • Tell Design Thinking, Lean Startup, Agile, Scrum, dual-track, and Waterfall apart by the job each one is for — not by popularity.
  • Separate discovery from delivery and say what dual-track is protecting.
  • Define cadence, scope, work in progress, and definition of done as control levers, not slogans.
  • Refuse to run a methodology as a costume on work it cannot see (a Waterfall Gantt on a complex bet; a Scrum theater on a one-way legal door).

Prerequisites

  • Lesson 1: loops, complicated vs complex, diverge vs converge.
  • You can name one live project and whether it has a paying or using human at the end.

Lesson Content

Methodology is a bet about information

A methodology is a pre-packed answer to: when do we learn, when do we commit, and how do we see unfinished work? It is not a personality, a tool vendor, or a moral identity. If you cannot say what information the method is designed to produce, you are collecting posters.

Design Thinking, Lean Startup, Agile, Scrum, dual-track discovery/delivery, and Waterfall all appear on the syllabus because teams mix them until none of them work. The field-guide move is to match the method to the information problem from Lesson 1. Complex product bets need discovery. Complicated known builds need sequence and a definition of done. Most real work is a sandwich: a complex edge on a complicated core.

Design Thinking and Lean Startup — two discovery coats

Design Thinking (as a method, not a vibe) is a structured way to stay in diamond 1 long enough to earn a problem statement, then to ideate and test. It is for work where the job-to-be-done is still slippery. Its failure mode is sticky-note theater: empathy maps with no decision, no prototype, no user.

Lean Startup is a structured way to treat a business or product idea as a hypothesis: build the smallest falsifying slice, measure a behavior, learn (pivot or persevere). Its failure mode is “MVP” as a synonym for “half-built product we already promised.” If there is no falsifiable claim, it is not Lean Startup. It is a launch with extra steps.

They nest. Design Thinking is strong at framing the human job. Lean Startup is strong at asking whether a market or usage bet survives contact. Using both in one quarter is normal. Using both in one two-hour meeting is usually a costume change.

Agile and Scrum — empirical delivery, not a mood

Agile here means the public values: individuals and interactions, working software (or working slice), customer collaboration, responding to change. It is a stance about late-breaking information. It is not a stand-up, a board, or a certification. If you cannot change the plan when the world talks back, you are not Agile no matter how many stickies you have.

Scrum is a named wrapper: a Product Owner who owns an ordered backlog, a Scrum Master who coaches the empirical process, Developers who own the how, a Sprint as a timebox, a Definition of Done, inspect-and-adapt events. Scrum assumes you can produce a done increment on a cadence. If your “sprint” cannot produce anything done — because the work is a single six-month regulatory filing, or because “done” means “we talked” — Scrum is the wrong coat. Cancel the theater; keep the inspect-and-adapt idea if it still fits.

Cadence in Scrum is a heartbeat, not a pressure pump. Compressing a sprint because a date slipped is how you destroy the only instrument that told you the truth about throughput.

Dual-track, Waterfall, discovery vs delivery

Discovery produces decisions about what is worth building: problem, demand, usability, risk. Delivery produces the thing to the definition of done. Mixing them in one stream is how backlogs fill with unvalidated guesses that then get “committed.”

Dual-track discovery and delivery is a way to run both without pretending they are the same meeting. One track is probing (interviews, pretotypes, spike tickets). The other is building what discovery already earned. The tracks talk. They are not two teams who never meet, and they are not one team that only delivers. If discovery has no capacity, delivery will invent the product in the sprint review.

Waterfall is a sequential bet: you can know enough up front to specify, then design, then build, then test. It is honest for complicated work with stable requirements (a known integration, a statute-shaped process, a construction sequence). It is dishonest as a moral opposite of Agile. The injury is using Waterfall documents to freeze a complex product bet, then calling every learning a change request.

Cadence, scope, WIP, and definition of done

These four levers are how any of the methods actually steer.

Cadence is a regular inspect interval (daily, sprint, monthly ops). It exists so learning has a clock. Random heroics are not cadence.

Scope is the work you are allowing into the current cadence. Unmanaged scope is how a sprint becomes a wish. Scope is cut or deferred; it is not “we’ll try.”

Work in progress (WIP) is how many things are started and not done. High WIP feels like productivity and is usually delay. A WIP limit is a decision rule: start nothing new until something finishes. Without it, dual-track collapses into eight half-discoveries.

Definition of done (DoD) is the quality bar for “this increment exists.” It is not a vibe and not “QA will catch it.” If DoD is missing, Scrum metrics lie, Waterfall test phases explode, and Lean “MVPs” ship as accidents. Write DoD as a checklist the team can fail.

A decision rule for the next project

  1. If cause and effect are known and the output is specified → Waterfall-ish sequence + DoD. Do not apologize.
  2. If you can ship increments and the backlog can change → Agile/Scrum cadence, protect DoD and WIP.
  3. If you do not yet know the job → Design Thinking / dual-track discovery before you “commit” delivery capacity.
  4. If the bet is “will anyone use this?” → Lean Startup measure, not a feature roadmap.
  5. If someone wants one method for the whole company → they are buying identity. Split the sandwich.

Uncertainty: vendor frameworks (SAFe and cousins) are outside this syllabus. This pack does not teach them and does not dummy-fire at them. If your org mandates one, still name discovery vs delivery and write a DoD.

Worked example(s)

Problem: A department wants “Agile” for a payroll cutover with a fixed legal date, plus a new self-service portal nobody has seen.

Steps:

  1. Split the sandwich. Payroll cutover is complicated with a one-way door → sequenced plan, DoD, rehearsal. Not a sprint-review toy.
  2. Portal is complex → dual-track: discovery this month (task success on the current pain), delivery only on earned slices.
  3. Do not put both in one Scrum board named “HR Digital.” That board will hide the legal date behind story points.
  4. Cadence: daily for cutover risks; weekly discovery reviews for the portal. Different clocks.
  5. Write two DoDs. “Payroll file matches the statute in a dry run” is not the same as “a user can complete a change-of-address without calling.”

Answer / result: Two methods, one department. Agile costume on the cutover would be malpractice. Waterfall on the portal would freeze the wrong screens. Dual-track is the honest org chart of the information.

Misconception inventory

MisconceptionReality
“Agile means no plan.”Agile assumes a plan that can change. No plan is just drift.
“Scrum is Agile, so any standup counts.”Scrum is a specific wrapper. A standup without a done increment is a meeting.
“Waterfall is always wrong.”It is honest when cause and effect are known. It is wrong as a freeze on a guess.
“MVP means ship a worse version of the full product.”MVP is the smallest test of a hypothesis, not a discount SKU.
“Discovery is optional if we are experienced.”Experience without contact still guesses. Dual-track exists because experience lies.
“More WIP means we are faster.”More starts usually mean later finishes. Limit WIP.
“Definition of done is for QA at the end.”If DoD is late, every metric lies.
“One methodology should cover the whole department.”Split complicated cores from complex bets. Coats, not identities.

Summary

  • A methodology is a bet about when you learn and when you commit.
  • Design Thinking and Lean Startup are discovery coats; Agile/Scrum are empirical delivery coats; Waterfall is a sequence coat for known work.
  • Dual-track keeps discovery from being eaten by delivery, and delivery from building unearned guesses.
  • Cadence, scope, WIP, and DoD are the actual steering wheel.
  • Costume mismatch (Scrum on a one-way legal door, Gantt on a product guess) is the usual injury.

Practice

Retrieval

  • What information does discovery produce vs delivery?
  • Name the four steering levers in this lesson.
  • When is Waterfall the honest coat?
  • What does Scrum assume you can produce on a cadence?

Near transfer

Your team runs two-week sprints. The board has 23 “in progress” items and no DoD. Which lever is broken first, and what do you cut this afternoon?

Far transfer

A school wants “Agile classrooms” and a Waterfall curriculum rewrite for a state exam. How do you split the sandwich without a holy war?

Spaced retrieval notes

  • Day 0: label your live project discovery or delivery (or both).
  • Day 2: write or find the DoD; if missing, write a five-line draft.
  • Day 7: count WIP. If it is over the number of people, name what you will stop.
  • Day 21: catch one methodology-as-costume (standups with no increment, MVP with no measure).

Difficulty tiers

  • Novice: discovery vs delivery + DoD + WIP.
  • Working: match all six named methods to jobs; split a sandwich.
  • Expert: run dual-track without letting delivery starve discovery, and without letting discovery never decide.

Domain scaffolds

Professional teams: put the method name on the brief only after the information problem. Safety, payroll, and legal one-way doors default to sequence + rehearsal even if the rest of the org is Scrum.
