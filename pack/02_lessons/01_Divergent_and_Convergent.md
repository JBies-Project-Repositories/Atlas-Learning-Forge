# Divergent and Convergent Thinking

## Learning Objectives
- Name diverge and converge as **modes of a room**, not as personality types, hiring categories, or moral identities.
- State what each mode is for: increasing live options versus reducing options against a named criterion.
- Catch simultaneous scoring (judging while generating) as the failure that kills diverge while pretending to save time.
- Put the current mode on the wall in language a late arrival could obey without a briefing.
- Run a 20-minute generate / 15-minute converge session and produce a clustered set plus a kill/keep list, not a winner of a status contest.
- Refuse a “we already know” converge that has no criterion, and a “let’s keep ideating” diverge that has already earned a decision.
- Hand the named mode to the rest of Arc A so the next hour can classify the work and pick a loop.

## Prerequisites
- You have sat in a meeting that produced either too many live options or a fake decision. No project-management credential is required.
- If the work can harm people (safety, medical, legal, money that is not yours), this lesson does not replace a specialist review. Modes do not license skipping a statute.
- Later Arc A lessons assume you can say, out loud, which mode the room is in. If you cannot, do not pick a loop yet.

## Lesson Content

### Mechanism: what the modes are for
A build loop only works if the room can **add options** and later **kill options**. Those are two different jobs. **Divergent thinking** increases the number of live options: more framings of the problem, more structurally different answers, more ways the current plan could be wrong. **Convergent thinking** reduces that set against a criterion you can point at: a constraint, a measure, a definition of done, a user-recognizable problem statement, a non-goal. The mechanism is a handshake. Diverge without converge is a lifestyle. Converge without diverge is a trial of the first plausible idea.

The move is for the next hour, not for your identity. You put a coat on because the weather changed. A room that cannot name the coat will do both badly at once: people will generate while defending, decide while exploring, and leave with a photograph of stickies and no next probe. Arc A’s job is to name the work and run a loop. You cannot name the work if the work is still a fog of un-killed ideas, and you cannot run a loop if you are still arguing about whether arguing is allowed.

Treat the modes as instruments. A diverge instrument produces **variety you could not have listed from the first speaker**. A converge instrument produces **a smaller set plus reasons**, including explicit parks and kills. If your diverge did not change the set, it was a recitation. If your converge did not change the set, it was a performance of confidence. Both failures are common on contractor-onboarding work, where the first plausible idea (“a nicer portal”) arrives before anyone has watched a person try to find the tax packet.

### Modes are not personalities
The same person should be able to do both modes in the same week. Teams that hire “creatives” for diverge and “operators” for converge often discover that the operators were never shown the problem and the creatives were never shown the constraint. That split is an org-chart costume. It produces two rooms that cannot handshake: a colorful room that never meets payroll, and a sequential room that ships the first idea that sounded like a plan.

People do have habits. Some talk first. Some score first. Some freeze until a senior person speaks. Habits are not types, and types are not permission to skip a mode. If you are “not a diverging person,” you are describing a skill gap or a safety problem in the room, not a temperament the atlas must respect. If you are “not a converging person,” you are describing a refusal to kill work, not an artistic calling. The field-guide rule is blunt: **coats, not identities**. You are not a diverging person. You are a person currently diverging, or currently converging, or currently faking both.

This matters because identity talk is how rooms dodge the wall. “I’m just a devil’s advocate” is undeclared converge during generate. “I’m a visionary, I don’t do constraints” is undeclared diverge during a legal cutover. “Legal is a no-person” is how you turn a constraint into a personality and then stop checking whether the constraint is real. Name the **mode**, not the **soul**. The wall does not care who you are. It cares whether the next twenty minutes add or kill.

### Divergent thinking increases live options
Diverge is not “be creative.” It is a production rule: **add before you score**. The output is a larger set of live options than the room walked in with. Live means still eligible for a later test or a later kill, not already loved. Wild sketches, How Might We notes, “what if we killed the form,” silent brainwriting, and eight bad drawings in eight minutes are diverge when they change the set. A round-table where each person restates the portal redesign is not diverge. It is chorus.

What the mode is for: stopping the first plausible idea from becoming the plan. On contractor onboarding, the first plausible idea is almost always a noun leadership already likes — a portal, a video, an AI helper, a welcome kit. If you do not force structurally different options (pre-fill the packet; make the packet findable; cut two forms; replace DMs with an office hour; do nothing to the portal and fix the email that buries the link), the first noun will eat the week. Diverge exists to make that eating visible and optional.

Diverge has a clock and a cancel rule. If the room cannot defer judgment — because a senior person is present to “steer,” because the calendar allowed twelve minutes, because someone is taking minutes that only record the ideas they like — cancel. A fake diverge poisons later critique: people remember being scored and stop bringing the weird option that was actually the test. Quantity is a tell, not a religion. Thirty ideas that are the same idea with different nouns are not quantity. Three mechanisms that could fail in different ways are.

### Convergent thinking reduces against a criterion
Converge is not “be realistic.” It is a production rule: **kill, merge, or keep against a named criterion**. The output is a smaller set plus reasons. Reasons point at constraints, evidence, non-goals, or a problem statement a user would recognize. Taste is not a criterion. Rank is not a criterion. “We already know” is not a criterion until you can say what you know and how a user would recognize it.

What the mode is for: stopping exploration from becoming a lifestyle, and stopping a status contest from pretending to be a decision. On the same onboarding work, converge is how you park “new vendor” because it is a written non-goal, keep “findability of the tax packet” because five contractors could not locate it, and kill “welcome video” because it does not touch the DM problem. Without a criterion, the room will converge on the idea that was drawn most neatly, or the idea the director restated, or the idea that sounds like a demo.

Converge requires the criterion **before** the killing starts. If you cannot write the criterion on the wall, you are not ready to converge. You are ready to argue. A useful criterion is observable: “must be testable this week without a new vendor,” “must reduce staff DMs in the first sitting,” “must not touch the payroll file format.” A useless criterion is a mood: “must feel premium,” “must be innovative,” “must excite leadership.” Leadership can be a user of a demo. Leadership is not a substitute for a contractor who cannot find the packet.

### Simultaneous scoring kills diverge
The usual injury is running both modes in the same mouth at the same time. A room that brainstorms while scoring ideas in real time is not diverging. It is holding a quiet trial. Every “we tried that,” every “legal will never allow it,” every laugh that lands on the weird option, every “let’s be realistic,” is a score. Scores during generate do not save time. They shrink the set to whatever felt safe in minute two, which is almost always the first plausible noun.

Simultaneous scoring has a social engine. The highest-paid person’s “just a thought” is a score. The note-taker who only writes the ideas they like is a score. The engineer who asks for the architecture of a sticky is a score. The designer who starts polishing one sketch while others are still adding is a score. None of these people need to be villains. The protocol failed. The protocol’s job is to make scoring **illegal for a named interval**, then mandatory for a named interval.

A second injury is the mirror: a room that “decides” without a criterion is not converging. It is performing confidence. Voting with dots on slogans is this injury in costume. The popular slogan is often the most familiar, not the most informative. If you must vote, vote later on **which assumption to test**, not on which mockup is prettiest. Familiarity is not evidence. A portal mockup that looks like last year’s HR transformation will win a simultaneous-score room every time, including when the actual failure is an email subject line that says “Welcome!” and hides the tax packet.

### Decision rule: name the mode on the wall
Decision rule, not a personality test:

1. If the set of live options is smaller than three **structurally different** answers, you are not allowed to converge yet. Diverge.
2. If the set is large and you cannot name a criterion, you are not allowed to generate more. Write the criterion, then converge.
3. If people are scoring while generating, stop the clock. You are not in diverge. You are in a trial. Reset or cancel.
4. If people are adding options after a criterion was named and a kill/keep pass already ran, stop the clock. You are not in converge. You are reopening the trial. Park the new option for a later diverge, or explicitly restart.
5. If a late arrival cannot tell which mode is legal by reading the wall, the wall failed. Rewrite it in one sentence: “Until :40 we only add. After :40 we only kill, merge, or keep against [criterion].”

The wall is the whole method. Sticky-note supplies are not the method. A timer is not the method unless it is coupled to a verb. “Brainstorm 20 min” is a calendar lie if scoring is legal. “Decide 15 min” is a calendar lie if the criterion is still “what leadership likes.” Write the verb and the criterion. Then obey them in front of people who outrank you. If you cannot obey them in front of people who outrank you, you do not have a mode problem. You have a safety problem, and the honest move is to cancel rather than run a fake.

Name the mode **every time the room changes jobs**, including inside a single meeting. Generate, then cluster (a light converge on sameness, not on merit), then score. Those are three jobs. Clustering by mechanism is allowed during the seam because it reduces duplicate nouns without killing mechanisms. Scoring mechanisms during clustering is simultaneous scoring in a nicer shirt.

### Facilitation script: twenty generate, fifteen converge
Run this as a 35-minute block plus a 5-minute close. One facilitator owns the clock and the wall. The facilitator may add options during generate and may not own the winner during converge. A senior person may participate. A senior person may not close during generate.

**0:00–2:00 — Wall and criterion draft.** Write two lines. Line 1: “Until minute 22 we only add. No scoring, no architecture, no ‘we tried that.’” Line 2: the **draft** converge criterion, visible early so people do not smuggle it as sniping: e.g. “After minute 22 we keep only options that could be tested this week without a new vendor and that touch unassisted tax-packet completion.” If the group cannot live with that criterion, you have two minutes to edit it. You do not have two minutes to debate solutions.

**2:00–7:00 — Silent add.** Everyone writes. No talking. Two to five options each. Silent add exists to break the first-speaker monopoly. If someone talks, the facilitator resets the silent clock, once. A second talk is a cancel.

**7:00–22:00 — Spoken add, still no score.** People read their options onto a shared surface. Others may only add: “another option is…” Questions go on a parking list labeled LATER. “Legal will never” goes on the parking list, not into a trial. The facilitator repeats, as needed: “That is a score. Park it.” Quantity target is not a number of stickies. Target is **at least three mechanisms**. If at minute 18 you have twenty stickies and one mechanism, prompt a constraint on purpose: “now add an option that does not use a screen,” or “now add an option that kills a form.”

**22:00–27:00 — Cluster by mechanism, not by wording.** “Chatbot” and “in-form helper” might be the same job. “Pre-fill from HRIS” and “find the packet” are not. Merge duplicates. Do not kill. This is the seam, not converge-on-merit.

**27:00–37:00 — Converge against the wall criterion.** Now scoring is mandatory. For each cluster: keep, park, or kill, with a reason that points at the criterion. Devil’s-advocate is legal here and illegal before. “Assume pre-fill is wrong 30% of the time — then what?” is a converge move. Pick **what to test or what to specify**, not what to ship. If leadership is in the room, they close only at the end of this block, against the same criterion.

**37:00–40:00 — Close the artifact.** Read back: kept clusters, parked constraints to verify (including the legal scare), killed items with reasons, and the one-sentence mode for the rest of the week. Photographing the board is not the artifact. The artifact is the list plus reasons.

If the room is larger than seven, split generate into parallel silent pages and only merge at the seam. If the room has a note-taker, the note-taker captures **all** adds during generate, not a curated set. If the clock is actually twelve minutes, cancel. Twelve minutes of simultaneous scoring is worse than no session, because it launders a pre-decision as “we brainstormed.”

### Failure catalog
**Scoring in the generate block.** The tell is a verb: tried, never, realistic, already, leadership won’t, architecture, estimate, “I like.” Any of those during add is a score. Reset or cancel.

**Criterion-free converge.** Dots on slogans. A director restating one option as “the way.” A vote on prettiness. A “decision” that cannot be executed by a competent teammate who missed the meeting. If the reason cannot be written next to the kill, it was not converge.

**Identity split.** Creatives in one room, operators in another, handshake never happens. Operators receive a poster. Creatives never see the tax statute. Both groups then blame personality.

**Chorus diverge.** Many nouns, one mechanism. The portal, the hub, the experience center, the one-stop shop — four labels, one idea. Variety was not produced. The first plausible noun ate the clock.

**Lifestyle diverge.** The criterion exists, the set is large enough, and someone wants “one more session” because killing feels like a vibe death. This is how onboarding programs run for eighteen months without a packet a contractor can finish.

**Fake wall.** The slide says DIVERGE and the senior person still steers in minute two. The wall that cannot be obeyed is decoration. Cancel is the professional move.

**Minute-taking as scoring.** Only the “good” ideas survive into the doc. The weird option that was the actual test never happened, according to the record.

**Seam collapse.** Clustering becomes killing. “These are all the same, so we just do the portal” is a converge smuggled into the seam.

Uncertainty kept in the body: popular brainstorm origin stories are contested and often caricatured. This lesson teaches the **enforceable** mode split (add, then kill against a criterion) as a professional move. It does not settle who invented a circle, and it does not recruit a lab study the syllabus does not contain.

### Handoff: from named mode to named work
Arc A’s job is not to admire modes. It is to **name the work** and **pick and run a loop**. Modes are the door. If you cannot say whether this hour adds or kills, you will mis-file the work as complicated or complex (Lesson 2), skip Check or Orient (Lesson 3), and Build before you can say what would change your mind (Lesson 4). The handshake is the whole atlas in miniature: increase options when the problem is still unnamed; reduce options when a criterion exists; never do both with the same mouth.

After a clean 20/15 you should be able to write four lines for the next owner: the mode you were in; the criterion you used; the kept clusters; the parked scare that still needs a fact (legal, payroll, identity provider). Those four lines are the input to classifying the sandwich. Findability of a tax packet is often still a problem, not a plan. The payroll file format is often already a plan, not a problem. Mixing them in one diverge is how you prototype a statute.

Do not collect facilitation tricks. If the wall named the mode and the clock obeyed the wall, you ran the move. If not, you ran a meeting. The rest of this mini-pack assumes the move.

## Worked example(s)
**Problem:** Leadership asked a mixed trio (ops, design, engineering) to “fix contractor onboarding” in time for a Friday leadership demo. The visible pain: new contractors cannot finish the **tax packet** without Slack-DMing staff. Two people already like a portal redesign. One engineer has a pre-fill architecture. Nobody has watched a contractor try to **find** the packet. A director will sit in “to help prioritize.” The trio has 40 minutes today and a week of calendar after that.

**Steps:**
1. Refuse identity talk. The director is not “the decider personality” during generate. Ops is not “the no-person.” Design is not “the diverge.” Write the wall: until minute 22 we only add; after minute 22 we only kill, merge, or keep against a criterion.
2. Draft the criterion in public before generate, so it cannot smuggle in as sniping: keep only options that (a) can be tested this week without a new vendor, and (b) touch unassisted completion of the tax packet in one sitting. Edit once. Do not debate the portal.
3. Silent add, five minutes. Each person writes at least two options. Actual adds from this room: make the packet findable from the welcome email; pre-fill from the existing HRIS; cut two redundant fields; replace DMs with a 20-minute office hour; a portal with a progress bar; an AI helper in Slack; a welcome video; print the packet and FedEx it; do nothing to UI and change the email subject to “Tax packet — 25 minutes, due day one.”
4. Spoken add to minute 22. Park “legal will never allow pre-fill” on LATER. Park “leadership wants a portal they can click” on LATER. Do not score. Prompt at minute 18 because the board is mostly portal nouns: “add one option that does not use a screen.” Office hour and FedEx stay live. AI helper is marked as same mechanism as “helper at the moment of stuck,” not as a separate miracle.
5. Cluster by mechanism: (A) findability of the existing packet, (B) pre-fill / fewer fields, (C) human unblock with a clock so DMs die, (D) new surface (portal / video / AI) that still assumes the contractor can find a thing. D is one cluster, not three ideas.
6. Converge 15 minutes against the wall criterion. Kill D for this week: a new surface is not testable without a vendor-shaped project, and it does not yet have evidence that findability is solved. Park legal-on-pre-fill as a fact to check, not as a veto from a personality. Keep A as the first test (contractors cannot complete what they cannot find). Keep B as a candidate test if A is not the whole pain. Keep C as a fallback operations coat, not as a product vision.
7. Close the artifact for the rest of Arc A: mode this hour was diverge then converge; criterion as written; kept A/B/C; parked legal scare; non-goal still “no new vendor”; Friday demo is not allowed to reopen generate. The director closes by accepting the criterion, not by restoring the portal.

**Answer / result:** The Friday artifact is not a portal mock. It is a named mode plus a smaller set: **findability first**, pre-fill second, office hour as operations fallback. Simultaneous scoring did not get to elect the portal in minute two. Lesson 2 will classify A as likely complex (users, mail, trust) and the tax-form contents as likely complicated (statute). Lesson 3 will put a Check on completion. Lesson 4 will refuse to Build a demo before Learn is written. The 20/15 did not ship onboarding. It made the work nameable.

## Key Terms
| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Divergent thinking | Mode that increases the number of live options; add before score |
| Convergent thinking | Mode that reduces options against a named criterion; kill, merge, or keep |
| Live option | An idea still eligible for a later test or a later kill; not already loved |
| Criterion | Observable wall used to kill or keep: constraint, measure, non-goal, user-recognizable problem |
| Simultaneous scoring | Judging while generating; a quiet trial that kills diverge |
| Name the mode on the wall | One-sentence rule a late arrival could obey: which verb is legal until which minute |
| Structurally different | Options that fail in different ways, not the same mechanism with new nouns |
| Seam / cluster | Grouping by mechanism without merit-killing; the join between add and score |
| Cancel rule | Stop a fake diverge rather than launder a pre-decision as “we brainstormed” |
| Coat, not identity | The same people run both modes; hiring “creatives vs operators” is not the handshake |
| Parking list | Scores and constraints captured during generate for later converge, not used as mid-add vetoes |
| Handshake | Diverge produces variety; converge produces a smaller set plus reasons; both required |

## Misconception inventory
| Misconception | Reality |
|---------------|---------|
| “Some people are divergers and some are convergers.” | Modes are coats. The same person should do both. Identity split wrecks the handshake. |
| “Scoring while we brainstorm saves time.” | Simultaneous scoring elects the first plausible noun and deletes the option that was the test. |
| “Diverge means be creative and defer all decisions forever.” | Diverge has a clock. Converge is half the move. Lifestyle diverge is stalling. |
| “Converge means the senior person picks.” | Converge means kill/keep against a criterion. Rank is not a criterion. |
| “More stickies means we diverged.” | Chorus nouns are not variety. Count mechanisms that fail differently. |
| “We can skip diverge because we already know.” | That sentence is how the first plausible idea becomes the plan. Prove the problem is user-recognizable. |
| “A 12-minute brainstorm is better than nothing.” | A short trial launders a pre-decision. Cancel if judgment cannot be deferred. |
| “Clustering is deciding.” | Clustering merges duplicate mechanisms. Merit-killing belongs in the converge block. |
| “Legal / ops / finance are no-personalities.” | Constraints are facts to verify in converge, not identities that are allowed to snipe during add. |
| “The photo of the board is the output.” | The output is kept / parked / killed plus reasons, in language the next lesson can classify. |

## Summary
- Diverge adds live options; converge kills, merges, or keeps against a named criterion.
- Modes are coats, not personalities, hiring categories, or moral identities.
- Simultaneous scoring is a quiet trial. It is the standard way diverge dies.
- Name the mode on the wall in one sentence a late arrival could obey.
- Run 20 minutes generate (silent then spoken add) and 15 minutes converge (cluster, then kill/keep with reasons).
- Cancel a fake rather than launder a pre-decision as a brainstorm.
- The artifact is a smaller set plus reasons, ready to be named as work and handed to a loop.

## Practice
### Retrieval
- What is simultaneous scoring, and why does it fail as a time-saver?
- Write a one-sentence wall that names diverge then converge, including a criterion.
- Give one tell that a room produced chorus nouns rather than structurally different options.
- Why is “I’m a devil’s advocate” during generate a mode failure?
- What belongs on the parking list during the 20-minute add, and what must not happen to those items until converge?
- When is cancel more professional than running a short brainstorm?

### Near transfer
A director joins a 35-minute “ideation” on contractor onboarding, says “I don’t want to bias you, but legal will never allow pre-fill, so let’s focus on the portal,” and then asks everyone to dot-vote at minute eight. Name the mode failures. What do you write on the wall if you are allowed to restart, and what do you do if you are not?

### Far transfer
You are outlining a public talk. The committee wants “the talk decided” this afternoon and also “lots of fresh angles.” They score jokes as they are pitched. Map this onto diverge/converge. What is the criterion for converge, and what 20/15 would you run instead of a vibe argument about tone?

## Spaced retrieval notes
- Day 0: write the mode on the wall of a real meeting you will sit in (add vs kill, with a criterion).
- Day 2: catch one simultaneous score (including your own) and park it instead of letting it kill an option.
- Day 7: run or facilitate a 20/15 on a live problem; keep a kill/keep list with reasons.
- Day 21: audit one project for lifestyle diverge or criterion-free converge; name which failure it is in one sentence.

## Difficulty tiers
- **Novice:** You can tell whether the current hour is adding or killing, and you can spot a score during generate.
- **Working:** You can put a criterion on the wall, run 20/15, cluster by mechanism, and produce keep/park/kill with reasons in front of a senior person.
- **Expert:** You cancel fakes, split chorus nouns, keep legal scares parked until facts exist, and hand a four-line artifact that Lesson 2 can classify without a replay.

## Domain scaffolds
Professional building work: the first line of a brief, standup, or critique is the mode. If the artifact is a roadmap, a backlog, a design file, or a leadership demo, write **add** or **kill** and the criterion before the nouns. Safety-critical and statute-shaped slices still use converge-by-checklist; they do not get a diverge that pretends a tax form is optional. Contractor onboarding is a sandwich you will name in Lesson 2 — do not let a portal noun eat the tax packet before the mode is named.
