Brainstorming as a Protocol

Learning Objectives

  • Cancel or run a diverge session from an explicit go/no-go rule: a live How Might We, deferred judgment that can actually be enforced, and no HiPPO steering in minute two.
  • Treat brainstorming as a time-boxed protocol (mode, rules, capture, cancel), not as a personality type or a talent for being clever out loud.
  • Enforce deferred judgment and quantity-before-quality as room laws, including what to do the first time someone scores an idea.
  • Capture every raw item on a shared surface with a parked-critique list, without cleaning, starring, or assigning winners during generation.
  • Facilitate a 40-minute diverge with the mode written on the wall, and refuse a winner, a shortlist, or a “let’s be realistic” close at minute 40.
  • Name the only honest outputs of the protocol: a visible set, a parked-critique list, and a capture audit — not a decision and not a photograph of stickies.
  • Refuse a fake brainstorm that would poison later critique by teaching the room that weird options get shot in public.

Prerequisites

  • L01: the room can name diverge versus converge and keep them off the same mouth. If people still think “creative” and “operator” are identities, fix that before you book a room.
  • L08: a problem statement and a How Might We exist, written, with constraints and non-goals. If they do not, this lesson’s decision rule is cancel, not “we’ll figure it out in the brainstorm.”
  • You have a wall, a shared document, or another capture surface that everyone in the room can see. A private notebook, a laptop facing one person, and “I’ll type it up later” are not capture.

Lesson Content

What the move is for

A brainstorm, in this pack, is not a mood and not a talent show. It is a diverge protocol: a named stretch of time in which the set of live options is supposed to get larger, stranger, and more complete than the first plausible idea the room already likes. The move is for protecting the option that would otherwise die in minute two — usually the option that is hardest to say in front of the person who paid for the meeting.

That is a mechanical job, not a spiritual one. You run the protocol when diamond 2 (or the ideate door of a design-thinking spine) is the honest next move: the problem is named, the How Might We is live, and the injury to avoid is converging on last year’s solution because it is easy to defend. You do not run it to “get alignment,” to perform energy, to fill a retreat agenda, or to launder a decision that already exists. Those are other meetings. Calling them brainstorms is how the word became a joke.

The protocol has four working parts: a mode (diverge, written where people can see it), two generation laws (deferred judgment; quantity before quality), a capture system that survives the meeting, and a cancel rule for when the laws cannot be enforced. Methods (brainwriting, Crazy 8s, SCAMPER) sit inside that container; they are L10. If you skip the container and jump to a clever method, you still have a status contest with extra paper.

Protocol is not a personality

People treat brainstorming as “talk in a room until someone is clever.” That is not a method. It is a status contest with snacks. The person who speaks first, the person who draws well, and the person who can joke at the director’s expense will fill the air. Everyone else will wait to see which ideas are safe. By minute ten the set has collapsed to the ideas that can survive eye contact.

A protocol does not care who is “a brainstormer.” Modes are not people. The same engineer who is quiet in a free-for-all can fill a page under brainwriting; the same director who is lethal in a review can generate if the rules make scoring illegal. When a team says “we are not a brainstorming culture,” they usually mean “we have never run a protocol, only a meeting called Brainstorm on the invite.” Culture is downstream of what you allow in minute two.

The test is boring on purpose. If a competent facilitator who does not work here could run the next forty minutes from a card on the wall — HMW, mode, two laws, capture owner, cancel condition — you have a protocol. If the session requires a particular charismatic person, or requires the boss to “set direction,” you have a personality. Personality sessions do not compound. They teach the room who is allowed to be interesting.

Decision rule: run, reshape, or cancel

Run the 40-minute diverge when all of the following are true before the clock starts: (1) a How Might We is on the wall in a form that allows at least three structurally different answers, including one that is not software; (2) deferred judgment is enforceable in this room with these people, including the most senior person; (3) a shared capture surface exists and a capture owner who is not the HiPPO has agreed to run it; (4) you have enough clock for actual generation — in this pack, forty minutes of diverge, not twelve minutes of “quick ideas” plus twenty minutes of undeclared scoring.

Reshape before you run when the HMW is close but dirty. A How Might We that smuggles a solution (“How might we build an AI helper for the tax packet?”) is a focus group on a decision already made. Rewrite it to the job (“How might we get a new contractor through required forms in one sitting without DMing staff?”) and then start the clock. Reshape is also the right move when the invite list is a review panel: take the director off “closer,” put them on “generator,” and write that on the wall. If they accept, you can run. If they want to steer, you do not run a brainstorm with a different name. You cancel.

Cancel if there is no HMW, if a HiPPO will steer in minute two, if capture is “someone’s notes,” or if the invite already says “pick the winner.” A canceled session is a professional act. A fake brainstorm is not free: people remember being shot down, and they stop bringing the weird option that was actually the test. You cannot buy that option back with a better whiteboard next month. Cancel, spend the hour on L08 (write the brief), and reconvene when the protocol can be real.

Deferred judgment as a room law

Deferred judgment means no scoring, no ranking, no “we tried that,” no helpful improvement, and no eye-roll during generation. The law is deferred, not abolished. Judgment is L10’s converge tools and L13’s critique. During the protocol it is parked, verbatim, on a list titled Later — not “bad ideas,” not “parking lot” said with a smirk.

The first violation is the session. Someone will say “that won’t scale” or “legal will never allow that” because they believe they are saving time. They are not. They are teaching the room the real rule: only ideas that already look fundable may be spoken. The facilitator’s job is to stop the clock for ten seconds, name the mode again, write the objection on Later, and restart. If the same person does it twice, especially if they are senior, end the session. A protocol that cannot survive its first HiPPO sentence is already fake; finishing the hour launders the fake.

Deferred judgment is not “be nice.” Niceness still kills. “That’s interesting — have you thought about the database?” is scoring with a smile. “Let’s capture it and keep going” is the law. Wildness is welcome because quantity needs variety, not because the pack believes in magic. You are not required to like the weird option. You are required to let it exist until a later, named converge can kill it for a reason tied to the brief.

Quantity before quality, without a lab coat

Quantity before quality is the second law. The job of the forty minutes is volume and variety, not a winner. Quality during generation is undeclared converge. It feels professional. It is how you leave with twelve restatements of the idea the director walked in with.

This pack teaches an Osborn-style enforceable subset: defer criticism, go for quantity, and keep combination/improvement for later (L10 clustering and SCAMPER). Those rules are old workshop practice, often caricatured, and this syllabus does not contain a lab study. We do not claim a measured creativity gain. We claim a mechanical outcome you can audit in the room: at minute 40 the board still contains options that would have died under live scoring, including at least some that are different mechanisms, not different nouns for the same mechanism.

How much quantity is enough? Enough that clustering (L10) has something to work on — typically more items than people, and more than one mechanism. A room of four that produces six stickies, five of which are “portal / dashboard / hub / app / tool,” did not diverge. They held a naming ceremony. The facilitator’s quantity check is not a quota on the wall that turns the session into a panic; it is a mid-point look: “We have one mechanism. We need two more families before we stop.” Then you keep generating, you do not start voting.

Capture rules that survive the meeting

If it is not visible to the room, it did not happen. Capture is the protocol’s memory. The usual injuries are a HiPPO who “takes notes” (they record what they liked), a facilitator who cleans while people talk (they converge in the scribe’s wrist), and a photo of stickies sent to Slack as the deliverable (the photo is not a set; it is evidence that paper existed).

Write the HMW as the heading of a shared document or the top of the board before generation. Appoint a capture owner who is not the most senior person and not the person whose idea is already the favorite. Every spoken item goes on the surface in the speaker’s words, lightly spelled, not improved. Do not star, color-code, or cluster during the forty minutes. Clustering is a later mode. A Later list sits beside the set: critiques, constraints-that-might-be-real, and “we tried that” claims, copied as sentences, not as verdicts.

At about minute 35, audit. Read the list aloud fast. Ask: “Did we lose anything that was said?” Ask: “Did anyone’s item get translated into a nicer idea they did not say?” Fix the record in the room. After the session, the shared list is the artifact. The photo is a backup. Names of authors are optional; names of “dumb” are forbidden. If legal or safety speech appeared, it stays on Later for a real owner in converge — it does not get to shut the diverge from the chair.

Failure catalog

The protocol fails in ways that still look like a good offsite. The room laughs, the board fills, someone says “great energy,” and the only idea that will be funded was in the director’s mouth before the invite. That is not a brainstorm. That is a ratification ceremony with markers.

Watch for these, and treat them as session-killers rather than as personality quirks. A failure catalog is only useful if it changes the next forty minutes, not if it becomes a slide.

  • The HiPPO (highest-paid person’s opinion) “just shares a thought” in minute two, and the room orients to it. If you cannot stop that sentence, cancel.
  • No HMW, so the board is a wishlist: nicer onboarding, AI, “make it pop.”
  • Twelve minutes of generation on a forty-minute invite; the rest is undeclared scoring dressed as “let’s be realistic.”
  • Capture that only records liked ideas, or a scribe who clusters and names winners while people are still talking.
  • Forty ideas that are one idea with different nouns (portal, hub, dashboard, command center).
  • Review tools during generation: Socratic grilling, devil’s advocate, “what’s the business model.” Those are L10 converge, later.
  • A photograph as the output, and no shared list that a stranger could cluster tomorrow.
  • Safety or legal used as a mid-diverge club. Park it. Then give it to a real owner in converge. Mid-diverge shutdown teaches people to stop seeing.

If two of those are true at minute ten, you do not “steer it back.” You name the failure, stop, and either rewrite the HMW or walk. Finishing a broken protocol trains the team to distrust the next one.

Facilitation script: forty minutes of diverge, mode on the wall

The 40-minute diverge is a generation container. It does not pick a prototype. It does not cluster. It does not devil’s-advocate. Those are L10, on a different clock (15 / 10 / 10 / 5) or a later hour. Mixing them is the usual failed offsite: the room thinks it diverged because it talked fast, then it voted on slogans.

Put the mode on the wall in letters large enough to point at: DIVERGE — we only add. Scoring is later. Put the HMW under it. Put Later to the side. Name the capture owner. Name the senior person’s role: generator, not closer. Then run the clock. A method from L10 may sit inside the container (brainwriting for the first ten minutes is a good default when a few voices usually fill the air), but the facilitator’s job in L09 is the protocol, not the menu of games.

Minute-by-minute:

  • 0:00–0:05 — Cancel check, out loud. Read the HMW. Ask: does this allow three structurally different answers? If it names a solution, rewrite or cancel. Ask the HiPPO: “Until minute 40 you generate; you do not close. If that is not acceptable, we stop now.” If they joke and do not agree, cancel. Point at the mode line.
  • 0:05–0:08 — Laws and capture. Deferred judgment; quantity before quality; Later list for critiques; shared surface; no stars. “If you hear scoring, we stop for ten seconds and write it on Later.”
  • 0:08–0:12 — Silent start. Two minutes of quiet writing into the shared doc or onto stickies, even if you will talk after. This is not yet L10 brainwriting as a full method; it is a protocol move to break the first-speaker monopoly.
  • 0:12–0:32 — Generate. One conversation or one pass around the table. Facilitator writes or watches the capture owner. Every “that won’t work” goes to Later, verbatim. Mid-point check at 0:22: how many mechanisms, not how many stickies. If one family, prompt for a different wall (“what if there is no new screen,” “what if we kill a form”) without scoring the previous set.
  • 0:32–0:37 — Capture audit. Read the list. Restore lost items. Strip improvements the scribe added. Confirm Later has the critiques, not the set.
  • 0:37–0:40 — Close the mode. Say the sentence: “We are done diverging. There is no winner today.” Name when clustering happens (L10). Thank the HiPPO for not closing. Do not take a “quick favorite.” Do not dot-vote. End on time.

If the senior person steers at 0:14, use the one-warning rule, then end. A 20-minute honest cancel beats a 40-minute fake. Afterward, send the shared list, not a recap that “the group leaned toward” the director’s idea.

What leaves the room, and what must not

The only honest outputs are: (1) a visible set in a shared place, still messy; (2) a Later list of critiques and possible constraints; (3) a capture audit (“this is complete as of 14:40”); and (4) a scheduled next mode — cluster and review, not “we’ll think about it.” That is enough to hand L10. It is not enough to brief a vice president as a decision.

What must not leave: a winner, a shortlist that was actually a HiPPO nod, a cleaned deck of the “best” five, or a story that the room aligned. Alignment was L08. Selection is converge. If someone needs a story for leadership, the true story is “we generated against this HMW; we did not pick.” If that sentence is unsayable, you were never going to be allowed to diverge, and the cancel rule should have fired at the gate.

Uncertainty: Osborn-style rules are a professional ancestor, not a result this pack cites from a laboratory. “HiPPO” is industry slang for a power problem, not a psychometric type. This lesson teaches the enforceable subset — defer judgment, quantity, separate review, cancel when power will not allow it — and leaves branded workshop choreography on the table.

Worked example(s)

Problem: The L08 brief is live: purpose is that new contractors finish required forms without DMing staff; end state is one sitting, on a phone, zero staff messages in a trial of five; non-goal is no new vendor this round; success is 4/5 unassisted. The How Might We is “How might we get a new contractor through required forms in one sitting without DMing staff?” Operations has booked forty minutes with four builders and a senior director who “has ideas” and already likes an AI helper that pre-fills the tax packet. The invite subject is Brainstorm — pick an approach.

Steps:

  1. Gate check before anyone sits. The HMW is job-shaped; keep it. The invite says “pick an approach” — that is a converge smuggle. Facilitator rewrites the agenda on the wall: 40-minute diverge, no pick. If the director needs a pick today, cancel and book L10 separately. Today they agree to generate.
  2. Name the HiPPO role. Director is a generator until 0:40. Facilitator says the cancel sentence: if they close in minute two, the session ends. Capture owner is the operations coordinator, not the director, not the designer who already sketched a helper.
  3. Mode on the wall. DIVERGE — we only add. Later column opened. Shared doc titled with the HMW. Two minutes of silent writing so the director is not first speaker.
  4. Generate for twenty real minutes. Items that appear: a single link in the offer email; a paper map of the current maze; pre-fill from HRIS; an in-form helper that does not open Slack; office hours so DMs die; kill two of the forms; a postage-paid paper packet; a human concierge for week one; SMS with the three fields that actually block people. Director’s AI helper goes on the list as one item, in their words, without stars.
  5. Park, do not try. “Legal will never allow pre-fill” goes on Later, verbatim. “We tried a FAQ” goes on Later. Neither is allowed to kill an item during diverge.
  6. Mid-point mechanism check. Nouns include helper, portal, FAQ, AI. Mechanisms so far: help at the moment of stuck, pre-fill before day one, human substitution, remove work. Facilitator asks for a family that is not a surface: findability of the packet itself. Two more items appear: put the packet URL in the same email as the start date; stop sending the forms as a zip nested under three logins.
  7. Audit and refuse a winner. At 0:35 the list is read back. One spoken item (postage-paid packet) had been “cleaned” into “better UX”; restore the original. Close: no favorite, no dots, no “we should really do the helper.” Send the doc. Schedule L10.

Answer / result: The protocol produced a visible set with more than one mechanism, a Later list with a legal scare and a “we tried that,” and a director who did not close. It did not produce a winner, and it did not ratify the AI helper. Findability is now in the set — it would have been invisible if the helper had been allowed to steer in minute two. That is the whole point of the protocol. L10 will cluster by mechanism. L12 will rank assumptions. Today’s job was to keep the option alive.

Summary

  • Brainstorming is a diverge protocol, not a personality and not a talent show.
  • Check three gates before the clock: live HMW, deferred judgment, HiPPO as generator not closer. Any fail → cancel.
  • Deferred judgment and quantity before quality are room laws. The first scoring sentence is the session.
  • Capture is shared, verbatim, unaudited until minute 35. Later holds critiques. Photos are backups.
  • Facilitate forty minutes of only adding, with the mode on the wall. Do not pick a winner.
  • Honest output: visible set + Later list + capture audit + a scheduled next mode.
  • A fake brainstorm poisons later critique; a cancel is cheaper.
  • Methods (L10) sit inside this container. Do not skip the container.

Practice

Retrieval

  • Name the three cancel gates and what you do if one fails.
  • What are the two generation laws, in enforceable language?
  • Who must not be the capture owner, and why?
  • What four things may leave a 40-minute diverge? What must not?
  • What do you do the first time a HiPPO scores an idea? The second time?
  • Why is “let’s be realistic” illegal at minute twelve?

Near transfer

A 45-minute “brainstorm AI features” is on the calendar. There is no problem statement. The VP has a mockup. What do you do in the first five minutes, including the words you say if you cancel?

Far transfer

You are facilitating a mixed group of nurses and software engineers on a medication-handoff HMW. The attending physician will “just listen.” Which capture and HiPPO rules do you set, and what is the cancel sentence if they start steering clinical plausibility in minute two?

Spaced retrieval notes

  • Day 0: take a past “brainstorm” and mark generate vs score vs capture. Circle the first scoring sentence. Would today’s cancel rule have fired?
  • Day 2: write a 40-minute agenda card for a live HMW, including HiPPO role and capture owner, without picking a method yet.
  • Day 7: sit in someone else’s ideation meeting. Log every undeclared converge. Count mechanisms vs nouns.
  • Day 21: cancel or reshape one fake brainstorm in the wild. Note what the room did after you refused to run it.

Difficulty tiers

  • Novice: deferred judgment + quantity + time-box + HMW on the wall.
  • Working: run the three gates; capture audit; refuse a winner at minute 40.
  • Expert: facilitate with a HiPPO in the room without collapsing the set; cancel cleanly when the second scoring sentence lands.

Domain scaffolds

Professional product sessions: HMW at the top of the shared doc; capture in that doc, not in one notebook; Later list for legal/security/privacy speech; a real owner for those items in converge, not a mid-diverge veto from whoever is loudest. Safety-critical work (dosing, aviation, one-way physical doors): the protocol still defers idea-scoring, but a facilitator may freeze generation if someone is about to run a live dangerous action. That freeze is a safety stop, not a taste judgment — write why on Later and hand it to the safety owner. Complicated compliance work (tax packets, filings): do not let “the statute already decided” kill diverge; the statute may constrain forms, not findability. Put statute claims on Later and check them against the actual text in converge.
