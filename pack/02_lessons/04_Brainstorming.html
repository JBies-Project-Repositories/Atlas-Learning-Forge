Brainstorming

Learning Objectives

  • Run a diverge session with deferred judgment and quantity-before-quality as enforceable rules, not posters.
  • Pick a generation method (brainwriting, Crazy 8s, SCAMPER, constraint-based, HMW) that matches the brief.
  • Use Socratic review and devil’s-advocate review as later converge tools, not as mid-diverge sniping.
  • Move ideas along a progression (raw → clustered → testable) instead of voting on slogans.

Prerequisites

  • Lesson 3: a problem statement and a HMW exist. If they do not, do not brainstorm.
  • Lesson 1: the room can name diverge vs converge.

Lesson Content

Brainstorming is a protocol, not a personality

People treat brainstorming as “talk in a room until someone is clever.” That is not a method. It is a status contest. A brainstorm in this pack is a time-boxed diverge with two hard rules: deferred judgment (no scoring, eye-rolls, or “we tried that” during generation) and quantity before quality (the job is volume and variety, not a winner).

If those rules are not enforceable — because a senior person is in the room to “steer,” or because the calendar only allowed twelve minutes — cancel. A fake brainstorm poisons later critique: people remember being shot down and stop bringing the weird option that was actually the test.

The brief from Lesson 3 is the input. Without a HMW, you are decorating. With a smuggled solution in the HMW, you are running a focus group on a decision already made.

Generation methods that actually change the set

Brainwriting (including 6-3-5-style rounds): people write before they talk. It reduces the first-speaker monopoly. Use it when a few voices usually fill the air.

Crazy 8s: eight sketches in eight minutes (or eight boxes on one sheet). It forces variety because there is no time to polish one pet idea. Use it when the problem is visual or structural (a flow, a page, a physical kit).

SCAMPER: Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate, Reverse. It is a prompt list for when the well is dry. Use it on an existing artifact you are allowed to attack, not on a blank problem.

Constraint-based ideation: add a hard wall on purpose (“it cannot use a screen,” “it must work in 30 seconds,” “budget is a stamp”). Constraints increase variety more than “think outside the box,” which is not a constraint.

How Might We (from L03) is the prompt, not the whole method. You still need a generation protocol under it.

Pick one primary method per session. Stacking Crazy 8s, SCAMPER, and a free-for-all in forty minutes is how you get noise.

Review is a different mode

Socratic review asks questions that expose missing who/job/constraint: “Who is this for on a Tuesday?” “What would falsify it?” It is not a quiz to humiliate the author.

Devil’s-advocate review deliberately argues the strongest case against a cluster: “Assume this fails in production — how?” It is scheduled, named, and time-boxed. If one person is always the devil, you have a culture problem, not a method.

Neither belongs during generation. Judgment mid-diverge is how quantity dies. Park questions on a “later” list. After clustering, run one review type, then stop. A room that Socratizes and devil-advocates and votes in the same half hour is converging in a fog.

Idea progression, not a winner

Raw ideas are not candidates. Progression:

  1. Capture — every item visible, no names of “dumb.”
  2. Cluster — group by mechanism, not by wording. “Chatbot” and “search that shows last config” might be the same job.
  3. Promote — a cluster becomes a testable claim: who, action, measure (Lesson 5 will prototype it).
  4. Park or kill — with a reason tied to constraints or non-goals, not taste.

Voting with dots on slogans skips step 3. The popular slogan is often the most familiar, not the most informative. If you must vote, vote on which assumption to test, not on which mockup is prettiest.

Failure catalog

  • The HiPPO (highest-paid person’s opinion) “just shares a thought” in minute two.
  • Someone takes minutes that only record the ideas they liked.
  • The session produces 40 ideas that are all the same idea with different nouns.
  • Review starts with “let’s be realistic,” which is undeclared converge.
  • No HMW, so the board is a wishlist.

Uncertainty: Osborn-style brainstorming rules are old and often caricatured. This pack teaches the enforceable subset (defer judgment, quantity, separate review). It does not claim a particular lab study from the syllabus.

Worked example(s)

Problem: HMW from L03: “How might we get a new contractor through required forms in one sitting without DMing staff?” Forty minutes, four people, one senior director who “has ideas.”

Steps:

  1. State the mode on the wall: 15 minutes generate, 10 cluster, 10 review, 5 pick a test. Director is a participant, not a closer, until step 5.
  2. Method: brainwriting 5 minutes (two ideas each, silent), then Crazy 8s on the two most different jobs (help at the moment of stuck vs pre-fill before day one).
  3. Ban solution-scoring talk. Park “legal will never allow that” on the constraint list for converge — it might be a real wall, and it might be a scare.
  4. Cluster: (A) pre-fill from existing HRIS, (B) in-form helper that does not open Slack, (C) a human office hour so DMs die, (D) kill two of the forms (Eliminate in SCAMPER).
  5. Devil’s advocate only on the cluster you might prototype: “Assume pre-fill is wrong 30% of the time — then what?” Promote one testable claim, not a winner of the offsite.

Answer / result: The output is one claim to pretotype (L05), plus a parked legal constraint to verify, plus a non-goal (no new vendor) already on the brief. The output is not 40 stickies in a photo.

Key Terms

TermDefinition
Deferred judgmentNo scoring or killing during generation
Quantity before qualityVolume and variety first; selection later
BrainwritingSilent written generation to reduce first-speaker monopoly
Crazy 8sFast multi-sketch diverge to force variety
SCAMPERPrompt list to attack an existing artifact
Constraint-based ideationAdded walls that increase, not decrease, variety
How Might WeProblem-shaped prompt that allows multiple solution types
Socratic reviewQuestions that expose missing who/job/constraint
Devil’s-advocate reviewScheduled argument of the strongest case against a cluster
Idea progressionCapture → cluster → testable claim → park/kill

Misconception inventory

MisconceptionReality
“Brainstorming is just talking.”Without deferred judgment and a method, it is a status contest.
“Quality in the room saves time.”Early quality is undeclared converge. You lose the weird test.
“The director should steer so we stay realistic.”Steering mid-diverge is how you get last year’s idea.
“Devil’s advocate during generation keeps us honest.”It keeps you quiet. Schedule it after clustering.
“Dot voting picks the best idea.”It often picks the most familiar wording. Vote on what to test.
“More methods in one hour means more creativity.”One method, done. Stacking is noise.
“SCAMPER is for blank-page problems.”It is for attacking an existing artifact.
“If we have fun we brainstormed.”Fun is optional. A testable claim is not.

Summary

  • Brainstorming is a diverge protocol: deferred judgment + quantity + one method.
  • Brainwriting, Crazy 8s, SCAMPER, and constraints are tools; HMW is the prompt.
  • Socratic and devil’s-advocate reviews are converge tools. They are later.
  • Progress ideas to testable claims. Do not ship a photo of stickies.
  • If the brief is missing, cancel the session.

Practice

Retrieval

  • Name the two hard rules of generation.
  • When do you use SCAMPER vs Crazy 8s?
  • Why is devil’s advocate illegal in minute two?
  • What is the output of a good session?

Near transfer

A team wants to brainstorm “AI features” with no problem statement. What do you do in the first five minutes?

Far transfer

Facilitating a mixed group of nurses and engineers: which generation method reduces the first-speaker monopoly, and how do you time-box the director?

Spaced retrieval notes

  • Day 0: rewrite a past meeting as generate vs review; mark where they mixed.
  • Day 2: run a 10-minute brainwrite on a live HMW.
  • Day 7: take 20 raw ideas and cluster by mechanism, not noun.
  • Day 21: catch one undeclared converge (“let’s be realistic”) mid-diverge.

Difficulty tiers

  • Novice: deferred judgment + quantity + time-box.
  • Working: choose the method; separate Socratic/devil’s-advocate.
  • Expert: facilitate with a HiPPO in the room without collapsing the set.

Domain scaffolds

Professional sessions: write the HMW at the top of the board. Capture in a shared doc, not in one person’s notebook. Safety ideas get a parked constraint list, not mid-diverge shutdown — then a real safety owner during converge.
