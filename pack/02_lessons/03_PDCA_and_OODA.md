# PDCA and OODA

## Learning Objectives
- Run PDCA as Plan–Do–Check–Act with a **named Check** written in Plan, not as a vibe after Do.
- Treat the Shewhart/Deming cycle as a professional **move** on an observable process; do not invent a canonical paper the pack does not have.
- Walk OODA as Observe–Orient–Decide–Act, with **Orient** as the Schwerpunkt (the scarce, model-updating step).
- Cite Boyd’s only paper, *Destruction and Creation* (1976), as the essay on breaking and remaking mental models; keep the loop itself in the later *Discourse on Winning and Losing* briefings.
- Distinguish after-action **model update** (Orient) from after-action **task-list update** (Act without Orient).
- Refuse “faster” as a substitute for Orient: speed without Orient is panic with a shorter cycle.
- Pick PDCA or OODA for a named slice of the contractor-onboarding sandwich and say what Check or Orient looks like this week.

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 1: the room can name diverge vs converge. PDCA Check is a converge instrument. OODA Observe can feed a diverge. Do not mix them in one mouth.
- Lesson 2: you can classify a slice as complicated or complex (and you will not Cynefin the people). PDCA is at home on observable processes; OODA is at home when the environment moves while you work.
- If the work can harm people, a fast loop does not replace a specialist review. Tempo is not a waiver.

## Lesson Content

### Mechanism: two loops, two clocks
A loop is a way to keep moving without pretending the map is the territory. PDCA and OODA are not competing religions. They are different clocks on the same spine: notice, act small, see, update. **PDCA** (Plan–Do–Check–Act) is a quality loop for a **process you can observe**. Its scarce step is **Check**: a comparison you named while you were still honest. **OODA** (Observe–Orient–Decide–Act) is a tempo loop for an environment that talks back while you work. Its scarce step is **Orient**: updating the model, including what you wished were not true.

The move is for the next week of named work, not for your identity. You are not “a PDCA person.” You are a person currently checking a process, or currently re-orienting a model, or currently faking both with a stand-up. Arc A’s job is to name the work and **run** a loop. Lesson 2 named the sandwich: payroll/tax cutover is complicated; portal/findability is complex. This lesson puts a clock on each coat. A dry-run file accept/reject is a Check. A leadership date that jumps twice in a week is an Observe that must Orient (“the bottleneck is findability, not the welcome video”), not merely add a task (“make the demo prettier”).

Do not collect diagrams. If you cannot say what would count as Check, you are not in PDCA. If you cannot say what in the model would change after a surprise, you are not in OODA. You are in a meeting.

### PDCA: the Shewhart / Deming move
Walter A. Shewhart taught a cycle for putting data to work on a process; W. Edwards Deming taught that cycle widely in quality work. Some quality writing uses Plan–Do–Study–Act because “check” in English can shrink to “inspect.” This pack teaches the **move** the syllabus names: **Plan–Do–Check–Act, with a named Check**. Origin stories for the diagram are contested. There is **no single canonical URL** in this atlas and **no invented paper** here. If you need a citation, cite the move, not a fake journal.

**Plan** a change small enough to reverse, and name the Check in the same breath: the measure, the comparison, the slice. **Do** it on that slice, not on the company. **Check** predicted vs actual on the measure you named in Plan — not on mood, not on whether leadership clapped. **Act**: adopt, adjust, or drop. Then the next Plan is allowed to be different because Check earned it.

What the loop is for: improving an **observable process**. Contractor packet completion, once you can see it: percent who finish unassisted in one sitting; time-to-find the packet from the welcome mail; staff DMs per new contractor in day one; dry-run payroll file accept/reject. If you cannot observe it, you cannot Check it. If you cannot Check it, PDCA is a costume.

PDCA fits **complicated** improvement and the operational half of **complex** work once a probe has produced a process. It is a poor fit for a brand-new problem that still needs diverge (you would be planning a solution to an unstated job). Use it when a process exists and you want it less wrong next week.

### Named Check is the scarce step
The Check is the part teams skip. They Plan and Do, then Act on vibes. That is not PDCA. That is busywork with an acronym taped on. A named Check is a **comparison**: what we predicted vs what happened, on the measure we chose while we were still honest. If you cannot name the measure in Plan, you are not ready to Do.

Write the Check as a sentence with numbers or observables, before Do:

- “We predict 4/5 trial contractors find the tax packet from the current welcome mail in under three minutes without a Slack DM. Check: task success on five recorded tries.”
- “We predict the payroll dry-run file is accepted on the first pass against the statutory layout. Check: accept/reject plus the first error code.”
- “We predict office hour cuts day-one staff DMs from ~8 per contractor to ≤2. Check: DM count on the next five starts, same window.”

A fake Check is a mood: “see how it feels,” “get feedback,” “demo to leadership,” “make sure stakeholders are excited.” Leadership can be an observer of a Check. Leadership applause is not the Check. A Friday demo that cannot fail is Act without Check.

Act without Check has a tell: the task list grows and the measure never appears. “Add a progress bar” after a demo is not Act in PDCA. Act would be: adopt the mail-subject change because 4/5 found the packet; adjust because they found it and still DMed on field 12; drop the welcome video because it did not touch the measure you named.

### OODA: Boyd’s essay and the later briefings
John Boyd’s **only paper** is *Destruction and Creation* (1976). It is an essay on mental models: you must break a failing concept (destruction) and build a new one that fits the world better (creation). The **OODA loop** as a four-word clock is not that paper. It lives in later briefings collected as *Discourse on Winning and Losing*. This pack will not invent a URL or a second paper. Cite the essay for the model-breaking move; cite the briefings for the loop. Origin stories for popular OODA cartoons are contested. Teach the **move**.

**Observe** the environment, including disconfirming facts: contractors still DMing; the packet link 404d; legal changed a field; leadership moved the demo; a competitor’s vendor pitch landed in the CFO’s inbox. **Orient** — the hard step — by updating the model with the new observation. **Decide**. **Act**. Then the environment has already moved, so observe again.

What the loop is for: **time** against a moving environment. Competitive settings, late-breaking information, a week when the map dies twice. It is a poor excuse to skip user research because “we need tempo.” Tempo on a false model is how you ship the wrong thing faster. It is also a poor excuse to skip a complicated rehearsal: OODA does not mean the payroll file gets to be improvised.

OODA is at home when Lesson 2 said complex, or when even a complicated cutover sits in a political weather system that changes the constraints. It is not a personality. It is a clock.

### Orient is the Schwerpunkt
**Schwerpunkt** here means the focal point, the scarce center the rest of the loop must serve. In OODA, that center is **Orient**. Observe without Orient is a pile of facts. Decide without Orient is a coin flip in a costume. Act without Orient is panic. Orient is where doctrine, bias, last week’s success, culture, and the 1976 essay live: you have to **destroy** the model that said “the portal is the problem” if observation said “they cannot find the mail,” and **create** a model that can actually decide.

Orient is not a vibe check and not a values poster. It is a written model update:

- Before: “Contractors fail onboarding because the portal looks dated.”
- Observation: 0/5 find the packet link in the welcome mail; 5/5 who are handed the link finish the statutory fields.
- After (Orient): “The bottleneck is findability of the existing packet, not portal chrome. Pre-fill is not yet the risk. Leadership demo of a portal would train the wrong model.”

That paragraph is Orient. The task list that says “fix CSS, add progress bar, schedule demo rehearsal” is **not** Orient. It is Act on the old model. Boyd’s paper is about that destruction and creation. If your Orient step is “we always do sprints,” you are not looping. You are replaying.

A practical tell after a surprise: did the team update the **model** or only the **task list**? Model update = Orient. Task-list update only = skipped Schwerpunkt. The atlas will keep using that tell in facilitation.

### Speed without Orient is panic
People quote OODA as “move faster than the enemy.” Speed without Orient is panic with a shorter cycle. You can Observe and Act every hour and get more wrong each hour. A contractor-onboarding week that ships three demo revisions after every leadership frown is not high tempo. It is a model that cannot be killed.

Panic has tells. The standup reports actions, never model changes. The board gains tickets after every meeting and never loses a belief. Someone says “OODA” to skip Check on a process that is sitting still. Someone says “OODA” to skip diverge on a problem that is still unnamed. Someone says “OODA” to skip analysis on a payroll file. Those are coats stolen from a briefing and worn as adrenaline.

Protect Orient with a clock, the way you protect Check in PDCA. After a surprise, a named ten minutes: what we believed, what we saw, what we now believe, what is therefore no longer worth doing. Then Decide and Act. If you cannot afford ten minutes, you are in Lesson 2’s chaotic domain (act–sense–respond to stabilize) and you should say so. Chaotic act is not OODA-as-lifestyle. Once the link is restored, Orient.

### Decision rule: which loop this week
Decision rule, not a personality test:

1. If a process exists and you can measure it → **PDCA** on a reversible slice, Check named in Plan.
2. If a competitor, stakeholder weather, or environment is moving faster than your meetings → **OODA**, and protect Orient as Schwerpunkt.
3. If you have neither an observable process nor a moving environment, you may still be in Lesson 1 (unnamed problem) or Lesson 2 (disorder). Do not pick a loop to look busy.
4. Complicated cutover work still gets PDCA (dry run Check) even if the program around it is politically noisy. The noise may also need OODA. That is two loops on two slices, not a mash.
5. You may use both in a month. You may not use both in the same meeting as synonyms. Mixing PDCA “check” language with a brainstorm, or OODA “tempo” language with a skipped model update, is how teams fake rigor.

Nested use is allowed when named. Example: OODA Orient says findability is the bottleneck; nested PDCA Plans a mail-subject change, Does it on five starts, Checks task success, Acts (adopt/adjust/drop). The outer loop updated the model. The inner loop improved a process. If you cannot say which is outer, you are collecting acronyms.

The rule is a coat for a slice, not a conversion of the department. A payroll dry run this Thursday can be PDCA while the same trio Orients on demo weather on Tuesday. What is illegal is using the words as synonyms in one standup so that neither scarce step is owned. If you cannot point at the Check sentence or the Orient paragraph on the wall, you did not pick a loop. You picked a vibe.

### Facilitation script: after-action Orient vs task-list update
Use this after a surprise, a demo, a failed dry run, a 404, or a week of DMs — any after-action that usually becomes a longer to-do list. Forty minutes. Facilitator owns the wall. The wall has two columns: MODEL and TASKS. Rule: **no new tasks until the model paragraph is written.**

**0:00–5:00 — Observe, facts only.** What happened, in language a skeptic could verify. “0/5 found the packet.” “File rejected, error code X.” “Demo date moved to Wednesday.” “Legal added a field.” No causes yet. No tickets. If people start solving, park on a holding list labeled NOT YET.

**5:00–15:00 — Destroy the old model (Boyd’s move).** Write the belief you walked in with, explicitly. “We believed the portal chrome was the reason contractors DM staff.” “We believed the file layout was already aligned.” “We believed Friday’s demo was a probe.” The group may only attack the belief with the facts from Observe. If the belief survives, say so. If it dies, write that it died. This is uncomfortable on purpose. Skipping it is how last week’s success becomes this week’s doctrine.

**15:00–25:00 — Create the new model (Orient).** One paragraph, present tense, that a competent teammate could use without you. Who fails, on what job, because of what we now think is true, and what is therefore **not** the bottleneck. Example: “New contractors fail unassisted completion because they cannot find the packet in the welcome mail. Statutory fields are not the current bottleneck. A portal demo would train leadership on the wrong problem.” If you cannot write the paragraph, you are not oriented. Do not Decide.

**25:00–32:00 — Decide against the new model.** At most three decisions. Adopt / adjust / drop in PDCA language if a process was under test. Kill, park, or keep in Lesson 1 language if options are still live. Decisions must be executable by someone in the room. “Be more user-centric” is not a decision.

**32:00–40:00 — Act as task list, now allowed.** Tickets may be written **only if they follow from the new model**. Each ticket points at a line in the paragraph. Tickets that would have been written under the old model are read aloud and, if they no longer follow, killed. This is the tell the atlas asked for: after-action Orient (model update) versus after-action task-list update. If the task list could have been written before the paragraph, Orient did not happen.

If leadership is present, they do not get a private Orient in the hallway afterward. The paragraph is the Orient. If the room is actually chaotic (link down, filing in three hours), do not run this script. Act to stabilize, then run it.

### Failure catalog
**Plan–Do–Act, Check skipped.** Measure never named. Demo applause as evidence. Feature factory with a quality sticker.

**Check named after Do.** You will pick the measure that flatters the Do. Name it in Plan or you are not in PDCA.

**Check on the wrong object.** Measuring stakeholder excitement for a findability problem. Measuring lines of code for a DM problem. Measuring demo polish for a file reject.

**PDCA on an unstated job.** Planning a portal because the problem was never diverged. Check will “pass” a thing nobody needed.

**OODA as speed cult.** Shorter cycles, same model. Panic.

**Orient as values talk.** “We need to be more Agile.” That is not a model of contractor findability.

**Task list as fake Orient.** Retros that only add tickets. Standups that only report busyness. After-actions that never kill a belief.

**Invented scripture.** A fake Shewhart URL, a fake Boyd journal article, a vendor OODA certification. This pack refuses them. Move, not relic.

**Loop mash in one meeting.** “Let’s OODA the PDCA brainstorm.” You skipped every scarce step at once.

**Using OODA to skip complicated analysis.** Tempo on a payroll file is harm. Sense–analyze–respond still applies to the cutover; OODA may apply to the weather around it.

Uncertainty kept in the body: PDCA/PDSA naming and OODA diagram ancestry are contested in quality and military-history talk. Boyd’s published paper is *Destruction and Creation* (1976); the loop is briefing material. Deming’s teaching emphasized studying results, not merely inspecting them. This atlas teaches the professional **moves**: named Check, Orient as Schwerpunkt, no invented papers.

## Worked example(s)
**Problem:** The sandwich from Lesson 2 is live. Payroll cutover (complicated) has a dry run on Thursday. Findability of the tax packet (complex) had five informal tries: 1/5 found the link in the welcome mail; the four who were handed the link finished the statutory fields. Leadership moved the demo from Friday to Wednesday and asked for “something clickable.” Slack DMs from stuck contractors are still the ops pain. A standup just produced twelve new tickets (progress bar, video, CSS, vendor call, extra rehearsal of the demo) and zero model changes.

**Steps:**
1. Name two slices, two clocks. Cutover is a process you can observe → **PDCA**. Demo weather plus the findability surprise → **OODA** first, because the model is currently wrong and speed on that wrong model is panic.
2. Run the after-action Orient script on findability before any new portal tickets. Observe: 1/5 found the packet; 4/4 finished fields when handed it; demo date moved; DMs continue. Destroy: the belief “portal chrome is why contractors DM” dies. Create: “Bottleneck is findability of the existing packet in the welcome mail. Statutory fields and payroll layout are not the current user bottleneck. A Wednesday clickable portal trains the wrong model.”
3. Decide from the new paragraph. Kill the progress-bar and video tickets for this week. Keep a mail-subject / link-placement probe. Park the vendor call (non-goal: no new vendor). Leadership demo becomes a **report of findability**, not a costume portal. That is Orient then Decide, not task-list growth.
4. Nested PDCA on the mail, Check named in Plan: “We predict 4/5 next contractors find the packet from the revised welcome mail in under three minutes with zero DMs to staff about location.” Do: change subject line and put the packet link in sentence one, five starts only. Check: task success and DM-about-location count. Act: adopt, adjust, or drop the mail change. Do not Act a portal because Check was inconvenient.
5. Separate PDCA on the complicated cutover, not mashed into the demo. Plan: Thursday dry run. Named Check: file accept/reject plus first error code. Do: the dry run. Check: the code, not whether the slide looked calm. Act: fix layout if rejected; do not ‘iterate’ the statute in a design studio.
6. When leadership frowns that Wednesday is “not a product,” Orient again rather than panic-Act. Observation: stakeholder weather wants a noun. Model: the noun is still not the bottleneck. Decision: show the 1/5 vs the next Check, in one page. Act: send the page. If you instead build a clickable costume overnight, you used speed without Orient.

**Answer / result:** The week’s loops are named and run. **OODA Orient** killed the portal-as-bottleneck model. **PDCA Check** on mail findability is written before Do. **PDCA Check** on the payroll dry run is written before Thursday. The twelve tickets are not the after-action. The model paragraph is. Lesson 4 can now put Build–Measure–Learn on a pre-fill bet **only if** findability’s Check starts to pass; otherwise Learn is still “they cannot find it.” Arc A is doing its job: the work is named, the loop is running.

## Key Terms

|------|------------|
| PDCA | Plan–Do–Check–Act: improve an observable process; Check is named in Plan |
| Named Check | Predicted vs actual on a measure chosen before Do; not mood, not applause |
| Act (PDCA) | Adopt, adjust, or drop because of Check — not “add more tickets” |
| Shewhart / Deming move | The quality cycle as a professional move; this pack does not invent a paper or URL |
| OODA | Observe–Orient–Decide–Act: a tempo loop whose scarce step is Orient |
| Orient | Updating the mental model after observation, including disconfirming facts |
| Schwerpunkt | Focal point of the loop; in OODA, Orient |
| *Destruction and Creation* (1976) | Boyd’s only paper: break a failing model and make a better one |
| *Discourse on Winning and Losing* | Later Boyd briefings where the OODA loop is taught; not a second invented paper |
| Model update | After-action Orient: a paragraph of what we now believe |
| Task-list update | After-action Act without Orient: more tickets, same belief |
| Panic tempo | Speed without Orient; shorter cycles on a false model |

## Summary
- PDCA is a quality clock for an observable process; the scarce step is a Check named in Plan.
- Shewhart/Deming is taught here as a move, not as a fake citation.
- OODA is a tempo clock; the scarce step is Orient, the Schwerpunkt.
- Boyd’s only paper is *Destruction and Creation* (1976); the loop lives in later briefings.
- Speed without Orient is panic. Act without Check is vibes.
- After-action: write the model paragraph before the task list.
- Pick one loop per slice this week; nest only when you can name inner and outer.

## Difficulty tiers
- **Novice:** You can write a Check in Plan and you can say that Orient is not speed.
- **Working:** You run after-action as model-then-tasks, nest PDCA inside a named Orient, and keep the payroll Check off the demo weather.
- **Expert:** You refuse invented scripture, kill tickets that belong to a dead model, and keep two clocks on the sandwich without mashing them in a standup.

## Domain scaffolds
Professional building work: put the loop name and the scarce step on the brief. Contractor onboarding continues: PDCA on packet findability and on payroll dry run, each with a named Check; OODA when demo dates, legal fields, or DM weather move, with Orient as a paragraph that can kill the portal. Safety-critical slices keep Checklists and Checks even when the political weather wants tempo. Do not OODA a one-way door.
