# Generation Methods and Converge Reviews

## Learning Objectives
- Pick one primary generation method (brainwriting, Crazy 8s, SCAMPER, or constraint-based ideation) that matches the brief and the room, and refuse a stack of games in the same hour.
- Run brainwriting to break a first-speaker monopoly, and Crazy 8s when the problem is visual or structural and polish is the enemy.
- Use SCAMPER only on an existing artifact you are allowed to attack, and use added constraints to increase variety rather than “think outside the box.”
- Keep Socratic review and devil’s-advocate review as **converge** tools — later, named, and time-boxed — never as mid-diverge sniping.
- Move ideas along capture → cluster by mechanism → testable claim → park/kill, and refuse to dot-vote slogans.
- Facilitate a 40-minute generate–cluster–review–promote session on a 15 / 10 / 10 / 5 clock.
- Leave with one or two claims that could be prototyped (L11–L12), not with a photograph of stickies or a popular slogan.

## Prerequisites
- L01: diverge and converge are modes you can name on the wall, not personality types.
- L08: a live How Might We, constraints, and non-goals. Generation without a prompt is decorating.
- L09: the brainstorm *protocol* is in force — deferred judgment, quantity, capture, cancel if a HiPPO will steer. This lesson sits *inside* that container and then **changes mode** into converge. If L09’s gates fail, do not start L10’s clock.

## Lesson Content

### What the move is for
L09 keeps the set alive. This lesson **changes the set on purpose**: generation methods change *which* options appear; converge reviews change *which* options deserve a test. The move is for leaving an hour with a **testable claim** (who, action, what would change your mind) instead of a pile of nouns. Diamond 2 without this lesson is a retreat photo. Diamond 2 with a winner-by-dots and no claim is a popularity contest that L12 cannot test.

Generation is still diverge. Review is converge. They do not share a mouth. The 15 / 10 / 10 / 5 clock exists so the room can feel the gear change: fifteen minutes only adding, ten clustering by **mechanism**, ten in one named review, five promoting a claim or parking it. If you let Socratic questions leak into minute three, you are running L09’s failure catalog with extra vocabulary.

Pick tools for a mechanical reason. Brainwriting is for airtime. Crazy 8s is for variety under time starvation. SCAMPER is for attacking a thing that already exists. Constraints are for forcing a different family of mechanisms. Socratic review is for exposing a missing who/job/wall. Devil’s advocate is for the strongest case *against* a cluster you might actually test. None of these is “creativity.” They are coats. Put on one generation coat, then one review coat, then stop.

### Decision rule: one generation method, then one review
**Choose brainwriting** (including 6-3-5-style rounds: several people, several ideas, several silent passes) when a few voices usually fill the room, when rank is uneven, or when the last session was a first-speaker monopoly. Silent writing is the intervention. Talking first is how you recreate L09’s status contest with a fancier name.

**Choose Crazy 8s** when the problem is visual or structural — a flow, a page, a kit, a sequence of doors — and the room will otherwise polish one pet sketch. Eight boxes, about eight minutes (or eight sketches in eight minutes). The starvation of time is the method. If the work is not drawable, do not force it; write eight *moves* in eight boxes instead, still against the clock.

**Choose SCAMPER** only when there is an **existing artifact** you are allowed to attack: a current form, a current email, a current packet, a current tool. Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate, Reverse are prompts, not a personality. On a blank problem SCAMPER becomes a random noun generator. On a live artifact it is a way to see the walls you have been treating as weather.

**Choose constraint-based ideation** when the set is collapsing into one family (“it’s an app”) and you need a different mechanism. Add a hard wall on purpose: no new screen, 30 seconds, budget is a stamp, must work at 11 p.m. on a phone, must work if legal forbids storing a copy. “Think outside the box” is not a constraint. A constraint is a wall you can point at.

**Then** pick **one** converge tool for the review block: Socratic *or* devil’s advocate, not both in the same ten minutes. If you must vote, vote on **which assumption to test**, never on which slogan is catchiest. If the HMW is missing or the HiPPO will close during generation, you do not pick a method. You cancel (L09).

### Brainwriting
Brainwriting is silent written generation, usually in short rounds, sometimes with sheets passed so the next person can add or combine without talking. The 6-3-5 pattern (six people, three ideas, five-minute rounds) is a named variant of the same move: **write before you talk**. Use it when the injury is airtime — directors, fluent designers, people who enjoy the sound of a room.

The mechanism is ugly and effective. Once an idea is on paper, it is slightly harder for the group to pretend it was never said, and slightly easier for a quiet person to exist. You still need L09 capture: the sheets go onto the shared surface, not into the facilitator’s pocket. You still need deferred judgment: no commentary between rounds. The round is for adding.

Do not “discuss as we go.” That turns brainwriting into a slow meeting. Do not have the HiPPO read their sheet first after the silent block; that reinstalls the monopoly at the reveal. Reveal by spreading every sheet, reading them in a neutral order (left to right, or a volunteer who is not senior), and capturing onto the shared list. Then, and only then, you may talk — and if the clock says you are still in the fifteen-minute generate block, you talk only to add, not to score.

### Crazy 8s
Crazy 8s forces variety by making polish expensive. Fold a sheet into eight boxes, or draw eight frames. Eight sketches (or eight structural moves) in eight minutes. The first two boxes will be the idea the person walked in with. Boxes five through eight are why you ran the method: there is no time to make them pretty, so they have to be *different* or they look like copies.

Use it on flows and surfaces: where does the contractor start, what do they see, what do they hold, what disappears. Use it when the last workshop produced one beautiful drawing and seven compliments. Do not use it as a personality test (“who can draw”). Ugly is in spec. Stick figures that show a *different door* beat a shaded mock of the same portal.

When the eight minutes end, people do not pitch. Pitching is scoring with a story. They hang the sheets, name the mechanism in five words if asked (“pre-fill before day one,” “find the link at all”), and sit down. Selection is later. If someone is embarrassed by box seven, that is often the interesting one. Capture it. Do not let them take it off the wall.

### SCAMPER, existing artifact only
SCAMPER is a prompt list for when the well is dry *and* you have a thing: Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate, Reverse. The artifact might be the current contractor email, the current PDF packet, the current HRIS screen, the current Slack DM ritual. Each letter is a question you ask **of that thing**, not of the universe.

Eliminate and Reverse are the prompts teams skip because they threaten someone’s project. Eliminate: which form, field, or login can die? Reverse: what if staff DMed the contractor with the three fields already filled, instead of the contractor hunting the packet? Combine: offer letter + packet link + start date in one artifact. Substitute: paper mail for the portal, or a human office hour for the chatbot. Those are mechanisms. “Add AI” is usually Modify with a brand name, and it is often the same mechanism as “helper at the moment of stuck.”

If there is no artifact, do not SCAMPER. Run brainwriting or constraints against the HMW. SCAMPER on a blank problem produces clever verbs attached to nothing, which L12 cannot test. If the artifact is sacred (“we cannot touch the tax forms”), you can still SCAMPER the *path to the forms*. That distinction matters in the continuing case: the statute may own the fields; it does not own the zip file nested under three logins.

### Constraint-based ideation
Adding a hard wall **increases** variety if the wall is real enough to forbid the default family. “It cannot use a screen.” “It must work in 30 seconds.” “Budget is a stamp.” “Works at 11 p.m. after a full workday.” “No new vendor” (already a non-goal on the L08 brief). The room that was about to draw a portal now has to invent a letter, a human, a deletion, or a pre-filled field in an existing system.

Soft constraints do nothing. “Keep it simple.” “Be user-centric.” “Think outside the box.” Those are posters. A constraint you cannot fail is not a constraint. Write the wall on the board as a sentence that could be false: “No new screen this round.” Then generate *under* it for a bounded time. You may later drop the wall if it was only a probe; you may not drop it silently during scoring because the director’s helper needs a screen.

Constraints are not the same as L08’s hard walls (regulation, safety, non-goals). Ideation constraints are **temporary loads** you add to move the set. Brief constraints are **real loads** you must not violate in a claim you promote. Keep the labels straight. Promoting a claim that violates a brief non-goal (“no new vendor”) is not creativity. It is a different project, and it needs a different brief.

### Socratic and devil’s-advocate are converge tools — later, time-boxed
**Socratic review** asks questions that expose a missing who, job, constraint, or falsifier: “Who is this for on a Tuesday?” “What would we see if this were wrong?” “Which constraint on the brief does this ignore?” It is not a quiz to humiliate the author, not a mid-diverge “have you thought about,” and not a way for the smartest person to own the room. Time-box it. Questions go at a **cluster**, not at a person. The author of a sticky does not have to defend their honor.

**Devil’s-advocate review** deliberately argues the strongest fair case *against* a cluster you might prototype: “Assume pre-fill is wrong 30% of the time — then what?” “Assume they never find the packet — then the helper never runs.” It is scheduled, named, and ended. If one person is always the devil, you have a culture problem, not a method: rotate, or write the anti-case as a group so the role cannot become a personality.

Neither tool belongs **during** generation. Judgment mid-diverge is how quantity dies (L09). Park questions on Later. After clustering, run **one** review type for ten minutes, then stop. A room that Socratizes and devil-advocates and votes in the same half hour is converging in a fog. L13 will add critique formats and pre-mortems; this lesson only needs the two named reviews as a bridge from a cluster to a claim.

### Idea progression: capture → cluster by mechanism → testable claim → park/kill
Raw ideas are not candidates. Voting on them is how familiar slogans win. Progression is the professional substitute for taste:

1. **Capture** — L09: every item visible, no names of “dumb,” Later for critiques.
2. **Cluster by mechanism, not by wording.** “Chatbot,” “in-form helper,” and “search that shows last config” might be the same job: help at the moment of stuck. “Put the URL in the offer email” and “stop nesting the zip under three logins” are the same family: findability. If you cluster by noun, you will promote a slogan. If you cluster by *how it would work*, you can test a claim.
3. **Promote a cluster to a testable claim:** who, action, measure. “New contractors can start the packet within five minutes of opening the offer email, unassisted” is a claim. “AI-powered onboarding” is a slogan.
4. **Park or kill** with a reason tied to constraints, non-goals, or a later test — not taste. “Park: needs counsel on HRIS pre-fill.” “Kill this round: new vendor, forbidden by the brief.”

Do not dot-vote slogans. Dots pick the most familiar wording and the prettiest sketch. If you must use dots, put them on **clusters-as-assumptions**: “which unknown should L12 test first?” That is a vote about ignorance, not a talent show. The popular helper can still lose to findability once the claim is written, because findability is upstream: if they cannot find the packet, pre-fill never runs.

### Failure catalog
Most failed generation sessions still produce a board. The board is not the tell. The tell is whether a stranger could cluster the board by mechanism tomorrow, and whether anything on it could be false. Slogans cannot be false. That is why they win votes.

- **Stacking methods** in forty minutes (Crazy 8s *and* SCAMPER *and* a free-for-all) as a creativity buffet. One method, done. Stacking is noise.
- **SCAMPER on a blank page**, or “AI” as a SCAMPER letter. You attacked a brand, not an artifact.
- **Devil’s advocate in minute two.** That is L09’s HiPPO with a debate-club coat.
- **Socratic grilling of a junior’s sticky** while the director’s helper goes unexamined.
- **Dot voting on slogans** (“AI helper” vs “better email” vs “portal”). Familiarity wins. Vote, if you must, on which assumption to test.
- **Clustering by noun** so “chatbot” and “helper” sit in different piles and “find the link” is not a pile at all.
- **Promoting a claim that violates a non-goal**, then calling it bold.
- **No fail language in the claim.** “Make onboarding nicer” cannot go to L12. If you cannot say what would change your mind, you have a poster.

When two of these show up, stop the review clock, return to clustering, and rewrite one cluster as a sentence that could be wrong. Do not add another method.

### Facilitation script: 15 / 10 / 10 / 5
This clock assumes L09’s gates already passed: HMW on the wall, deferred judgment during generate, capture owner who is not the HiPPO, director named as generator until the promote block. If those are not true, cancel; do not “adapt” the 15 / 10 / 10 / 5 into a steering meeting.

Put four times on the wall. Put the method name under GENERATE. Put “mechanism, not noun” under CLUSTER. Put one review name under REVIEW. Put “claim = who + action + measure” under PROMOTE. Then refuse to negotiate the clock because someone “needs more time to discuss.” Discussion is how the fourth clock disappears and the slogan returns.

Minute-by-minute:

- **0:00–0:15 — Generate (one method).** Example default for a mixed-rank room: five minutes silent brainwriting (two ideas each), then ten minutes of Crazy 8s on the two most different *jobs* that showed up, still without scoring. Capture everything. Later list for critiques. Facilitator’s only mid-point line: “We need another mechanism family, not another synonym.”
- **0:15–0:25 — Cluster by mechanism.** No new ideas unless they fill an empty family in five words. Group stickies/sheets under verbs of how they work: find, pre-fill, help-at-stuck, remove-work, human-substitute. Kill duplicate nouns into those piles. Do not name winners. Do not let “AI” be a cluster; ask which mechanism it is.
- **0:25–0:35 — One review.** Either Socratic on each cluster (“who, Tuesday, what falsifies?”) or devil’s advocate on the cluster you are tempted to build. Not both. Time-box per cluster (two minutes). Park legal/safety as questions for owners, not as taste. The director may now *review*, not generate — still not close.
- **0:35–0:40 — Promote or park.** Write one or two testable claims. Example: “New contractors can find and start the packet within five minutes of the offer email, unassisted.” Park the rest with reasons (“vendor non-goal,” “needs counsel,” “downstream of findability”). No dots on slogans. If the room wants a vote, vote which claim to test first in L12.

End on time. Send the claims, the cluster names, and the Later list. Do not send a recap that “the team is excited about the helper.” Excitement is not a claim.

**Uncertainty:** Crazy 8s, 6-3-5, and SCAMPER are named workshop moves in wide circulation; this syllabus lists them without a canonical paper. The pack teaches the **move** (silent writing; time-starved variety; prompt-the-artifact; add a wall; review later). It does not certify a vendor sprint and does not invent a lab result about which method “wins.”

## Worked example(s)
**Problem:** L09 left a visible set against the contractor HMW: offer-email link, paper map of the maze, HRIS pre-fill, in-form helper that does not open Slack, office hours, kill two forms, postage-paid packet, concierge week, SMS of blocking fields, “stop the nested zip,” and the director’s AI helper. Later holds “legal will never allow pre-fill” and “we tried a FAQ.” Forty minutes remain this afternoon. The director wants dots.

**Steps:**
1. **Refuse dots on slogans.** Write the 15 / 10 / 10 / 5 clock. Method for the generate block: the set already exists from L09, so spend the fifteen minutes on **constraint-based ideation** under “no new screen” plus a five-minute SCAMPER on the *current offer email and packet zip* (existing artifacts). Do not SCAMPER “onboarding” in the abstract.
2. **Generate under the wall.** No-new-screen produces: one URL in the offer email, postage-paid packet, office hour, SMS, kill-a-form, human paste of HRIS values (a concierge, not a product). SCAMPER on the zip: Eliminate the zip; Substitute a single form; Reverse — staff sends the three blocking fields already filled; Combine start date + URL. The AI helper cannot survive “no new screen” without becoming a human; write that down without scoring.
3. **Cluster by mechanism, not noun.** Piles: **(A) Findability** — URL in offer email, kill the nested zip, paper map of the current path. **(B) Pre-fill** — HRIS values, staff-pasted values, SMS of known fields. **(C) Help-at-stuck** — in-form helper, FAQ, AI helper (same pile; director can see the grouping). **(D) Remove work** — kill two forms, Eliminate fields. **(E) Human substitute** — office hours, concierge week. “Portal” is not a pile.
4. **Devil’s advocate only on the pile you might build.** The room is tempted by (B) and (C) because they look like product. Ten minutes: “Assume they never find the packet — then pre-fill and helper never run.” “Assume pre-fill is wrong 30% of the time — then what?” Socratic would also work; do not run both. Legal scare stays on Later for counsel; it does not auto-kill (B).
5. **Promote claims, park the rest.** Claim 1: “A new contractor can start the required packet within five minutes of opening the offer email, without DMing staff.” Claim 2 (parked as downstream): “If findability holds, pre-fill from existing HRIS reduces fields they type to under three, with a named error path when HRIS is wrong.” Kill “new vendor AI” this round as a non-goal violation. Park (E) as an operations choice, not a product bet.
6. **No winner of the offsite.** The output is Claim 1 to take into L11–L12, Claim 2 waiting on Claim 1, and a Later item for counsel. The director’s helper is clustered inside (C), not crowned.

**Answer / result:** Findability is now a **claim**, not a vibe, because clustering by mechanism separated “can they find it” from “does pre-fill work” from “does a helper help.” Dot-voting slogans would have crowned the AI helper. L12 will rank ignorance × impact and will likely test findability first; this lesson’s job was to make that ranking possible.

## Key Terms
| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Brainwriting | Silent written generation to reduce first-speaker monopoly |
| 6-3-5-style rounds | Named brainwriting pattern: several people, several ideas, timed silent passes |
| Crazy 8s | Fast multi-sketch (or multi-move) diverge; variety by starvation of polish |
| SCAMPER | Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate, Reverse — prompts for an existing artifact |
| Constraint-based ideation | Temporary hard walls added to force a different mechanism family |
| Mechanism | How an idea would work; the correct clustering key |
| Socratic review | Time-boxed questions that expose missing who/job/constraint/falsifier |
| Devil’s-advocate review | Scheduled, named argument of the strongest case against a cluster |
| Idea progression | Capture → cluster by mechanism → testable claim → park/kill |
| Testable claim | Who + action + measure; a sentence that can be false |
| Park | Keep without building; reason tied to a wall, a dependency, or a later test |
| Kill (this round) | Stop spending, with a brief-tied reason, not a taste reason |
| Dot-vote injury | Voting on slogans or pretty sketches; familiarity wins |
| 15 / 10 / 10 / 5 | Generate, cluster, review, promote — four clocks in one session |

## Misconception inventory
| Misconception | Reality |
|---------------|---------|
| “More methods in one hour means more creativity.” | One method, done. Stacking is noise. |
| “SCAMPER is for blank-page problems.” | It is for attacking an existing artifact. |
| “Devil’s advocate during generation keeps us honest.” | It keeps you quiet. Schedule it after clustering. |
| “Dot voting picks the best idea.” | It often picks the most familiar wording. Vote on what to test. |
| “Socratic review is just asking smart questions whenever.” | It is a converge tool, aimed at clusters, time-boxed. |
| “Clustering is grouping similar stickies.” | Similar nouns can hide different jobs. Group by mechanism. |
| “The output of ideation is a winner.” | The output is a testable claim, plus parks and kills with reasons. |
| “Constraints kill creativity.” | Real walls increase variety; posters do not. |
| “AI is its own cluster.” | Ask which mechanism it is. Usually help-at-stuck with a brand. |
| “We already generated in L09, so skip to voting.” | Without clustering and a claim, L09’s set is still slogans. |

## Summary
- Generation methods change the set; converge reviews change what deserves a test. They are different modes.
- Pick one primary method: brainwriting for airtime, Crazy 8s for visual/structural variety, SCAMPER for an existing artifact, constraints to force a new family.
- Socratic and devil’s-advocate reviews are later, named, and time-boxed. They are illegal in minute two.
- Progress ideas: capture → cluster by mechanism → testable claim → park/kill.
- Do not dot-vote slogans. If you vote, vote on which assumption to test.
- Facilitate 15 / 10 / 10 / 5. Mixing the clocks is the usual failed offsite.
- Leave with a claim L11–L12 can fail, not with a photo or a crowned helper.

## Practice
### Retrieval
- When do you choose brainwriting instead of Crazy 8s?
- Why is SCAMPER illegal on a blank problem?
- What four steps make idea progression, and where do dots go if they go anywhere?
- Why are Socratic and devil’s-advocate reviews converge tools?
- What does the 15 / 10 / 10 / 5 clock protect you from?
- Give one temporary ideation constraint and one brief constraint, and say how they differ.

### Near transfer
A team wants to “SCAMPER our roadmap” with no current artifact on the table, then vote with dots on the funniest verbs. What do you run instead, and what sentence do you write as the promote output?

### Far transfer
Facilitating clinicians and engineers on a handoff HMW: pick a generation method that reduces the first-speaker monopoly, a constraint that forbids a new app, and a 10-minute review that cannot become a junior being grilled. Write the four clock labels you put on the wall.

## Spaced retrieval notes
- Day 0: take 20 raw ideas from a live project and cluster them by mechanism, not noun. Count how many slogans you had been treating as clusters.
- Day 2: run a 10-minute brainwrite on a live HMW, then a 10-minute cluster. No review yet. Notice the urge to score.
- Day 7: take one cluster and write a testable claim (who, action, measure) plus a park/kill reason for the rest.
- Day 21: catch a dot-vote on slogans or a devil’s advocate in minute two; stop it and rename the mode.

## Difficulty tiers
- **Novice:** one method; deferred judgment during generate; no dots on slogans.
- **Working:** cluster by mechanism; run one named review; promote a claim.
- **Expert:** keep a HiPPO inside 15 / 10 / 10 / 5 without letting their helper become a cluster of its own; park legally loaded items without shutting the room.

## Domain scaffolds
Professional product work: existing artifact for SCAMPER is usually the current path (email, form, ticket), not a fantasy product. Safety-critical domains: devil’s advocate is required before promoting a claim that could teach a dangerous action — still *after* clustering, and with a safety owner in the review block, not as heckling. Compliance-heavy packets (tax, identity, clinical documentation): Eliminate and Reverse are legal questions for counsel, not mid-generate vetoes; findability and comprehension remain fair generation families even when fields are statutory. Mixed-rank rooms default to brainwriting first. Visual flows default to Crazy 8s. Empty-roadmap offsites default to cancel (L09), not to SCAMPER-the-void.
