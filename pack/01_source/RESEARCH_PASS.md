# Deep-research pass (2026-09-15)

Cited report from the session workflow `deep-research`. Status: **Partial** (paywalls and some 1950s books not fully inspectable).

## What the research confirmed (already in the atlas)

- Complicated → sense–analyze–respond; complex → probe–sense–respond (Snowden & Boone, HBR 2007).
- Orient is the center of OODA, not a fast sequential step (Boyd).
- BML intellectual order is Learn → Measure → Build (Ries).
- Double Diamond is not linear (Design Council).
- Scrum Increment requires Definition of Done (2020 Guide).
- Dual-track is two kinds of work, not two teams (Patton).
- Commander’s intent = purpose + end state so others can act without further orders (ADP 6-0).

## Accuracy notes for facilitators (do not overclaim)

1. **PDCA vs PDSA.** Deming later wanted *Study*, not *Check*, and called PDCA a corruption. This pack still uses PDCA as the named syllabus move, but **Check means compare prediction to result**, not hold-back or compliance.
2. **Crazy 8s.** Primary (Knapp *Sprint*): eight one-minute variations of *one* strong idea. “Crazy” is pace, not wildness.
3. **Looks-like / works-like / experience** is pack teaching. Houde & Hill’s primary triad is role / look-and-feel / implementation.
4. **Royce 1970** did not name “waterfall” in the inspected quotations. Sequential two-step analysis-and-coding can suffice for a *small* effort; a large-system plan keyed only to those steps is doomed.
5. **RAT** as a named gate is pack teaching, not a single inspected primary coinage.
6. **Premortem 30%** figure is Klein reporting Mitchell et al. 1989; the 1989 abstract was not independently confirmed at page level — do not treat 30% as a lab result in this pack.
7. **Launch checklists** are organization-specific (SRE is an example). Ours is a slice go/no-go, not aviation certification.
